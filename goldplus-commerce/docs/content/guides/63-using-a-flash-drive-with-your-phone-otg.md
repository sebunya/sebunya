Most recent Android phones can read a flash drive directly through **USB OTG** (On-The-Go). Plug the drive into the phone with an OTG adapter that fits your phone's port, open the Files app, and copy files across. No computer needed. It is a handy way to free up space or back up photos when you are away from a laptop.

# What USB OTG is

Normally a phone acts as the device, and the computer is in charge. OTG lets the phone take charge instead, so it can power and read a flash drive, a keyboard or a mouse.

Not every phone supports OTG, and some budget phones turn it off. To check:

- Look in the phone's specifications on the maker's website for **USB OTG**.
- On some phones, there is an **OTG** switch in Settings that must be turned on first.
- Or just try it with an adapter: if the drive appears in the Files app, it works.

# The adapter you need

Most flash drives, including ours, have a **USB-A** plug, the full-size rectangular one. Your phone's port is smaller, so you need an OTG adapter or cable that matches it:

- **USB-C phone:** a USB-C (male) to USB-A (female) OTG adapter.
- **Micro-USB (V8) phone:** a Micro-USB (male) to USB-A (female) OTG adapter.

A normal charging cable will not do this job; it has the wrong ends.

**iPhones:** iPhones with a USB-C port can open a flash drive in the **Files** app with a USB-C adapter. Lightning iPhones need a Lightning to USB adapter, and some flash drives need more power than the iPhone gives through it.

# How to copy photos to the drive (Android)

1. Plug the adapter into the phone, then the flash drive into the adapter.
2. A notification should appear saying a USB drive is connected. Tap it, or open your **Files** or **File Manager** app and look for the USB drive.
3. In the Files app, go to internal storage, then **DCIM**, then **Camera**.
4. Select the photos and videos you want.
5. Choose **Copy** (to keep them on the phone too) or **Move** (to free space), and pick the USB drive as the destination.
6. Wait until it finishes. Large videos can take a while.
7. **Eject the drive** from the notification or from Settings, then Storage, before unplugging.

> Do not unplug the drive while files are copying. Interrupted copies can leave broken files, and moved files that did not finish may be lost.

# Formats phones can read

Android phones generally read **FAT32** and **exFAT** drives. Many cannot read **NTFS**. If your phone does not see a drive that works on a computer, the format is a likely cause. Formatting erases the drive, so copy anything you need off it first, then format it as exFAT on a computer.

Remember FAT32 cannot hold a single file over 4GB, which can stop a long video from copying.

# Tips

- **Battery:** the phone powers the drive, which uses some battery. Copy large amounts while charging, or with a full battery.
- **Keep it steady.** A flash drive and adapter sticking out of the phone are easy to knock. Put the phone flat on a table while copying.
- **Watch for a power-hungry drive.** If the drive keeps disconnecting, the phone may not be giving it enough power.
- **One backup is not enough.** After copying photos off the phone, copy them to a computer or the cloud too.
