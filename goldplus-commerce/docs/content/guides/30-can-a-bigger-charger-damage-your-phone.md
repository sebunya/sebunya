A charger with more watts will not damage your phone. The phone decides how much power it takes, and a properly made charger gives only what the phone asks for. A 65W charger charges a phone that supports 20W at up to 20W.

The real risks come from somewhere else: fake or badly made chargers, damaged cables and heat.

# Why the phone is in control

Think of the charger's watt rating as the most it can supply, not the amount it forces into the phone.

- **Current is drawn, not pushed.** At a given voltage, the phone takes the current it needs. A charger that can give 3A does not force 3A into a phone that wants 1A.
- **Voltage is agreed first.** Fast-charging standards such as USB PD and Quick Charge start at a safe 5V. The charger only raises the voltage after the phone asks for it.
- **The phone has its own charging circuit.** Between the port and the battery is a chip that manages how fast the battery fills, and slows down when it gets warm or nearly full.

So the phone sets the pace. A bigger charger simply has headroom you are not using.

# When a bigger charger makes sense

Extra headroom is not wasted:

- One charger can serve a phone today and a tablet or laptop tomorrow.
- A multi-port charger with more total power can charge two devices with less slowdown.
- A newer phone you buy later may support more power than your current one.

# The real risks

## Fake or poorly made chargers

A genuine charger follows the rules above. A cheap fake may not: it can give an unstable voltage, lack protection circuits, or use thin wiring inside that overheats. These risks have nothing to do with the watt number on the box, and everything to do with how the charger is built.

Buy from a shop you trust, and check the product is genuine. Every genuine GoldPlus product has a code on its security hologram that you can check on our [verification page](/verification).

## Damaged cables

A frayed cable or a loose plug can heat up at the damaged point, and a poor connection can cause sparking at the port. Replace damaged cables rather than taping them.

If you use a high-power charger with a laptop or tablet, the cable must be rated for that power. Most USB-C cables carry up to 60W. Above 60W, USB PD needs a cable built and marked for 5A (100W or more). ## Heat

Any charging creates some heat. It builds up when the phone is under a pillow, in direct sun or in a thick case. Charge on a hard surface with air around it.

## Wrong voltage from non-USB chargers

All of this applies to USB chargers. A barrel-plug adapter from another appliance, such as a router or a speaker, may give a fixed voltage the device was never designed for. Never force a different adapter's plug into a device.

# What about battery life?

Fast charging produces more heat than slow charging, and heat is what wears lithium-ion batteries over time. Phones manage this by charging fast while the battery is low and slowing down as it fills. For most people, fast charging is fine for everyday use. If you charge overnight and are in no hurry, a slower charge does no harm either.

- The phone controls the power it takes.
