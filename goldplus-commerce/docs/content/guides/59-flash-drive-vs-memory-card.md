Choose a **memory card** if you want more storage inside a phone, camera, dashcam, CCTV recorder, radio or drone. Choose a **flash drive** if you want to carry files between computers, play films or music on a TV or car stereo, or keep a backup you can unplug and put away. Both store files; the difference is where they plug in.

# Memory cards: storage that stays in a device

A microSD memory card is tiny and sits in a slot inside the device, usually in the SIM tray on a phone.

**Best for:**

- Giving a full Android phone more room for photos, videos and music.
- Cameras, action cameras and drones.
- Dashcams and CCTV, which record onto the card non-stop.
- Radios and MP3 players with a card slot.

**Keep in mind:**

- Your device needs a card slot. Many phones, including iPhones, do not have one.
- Older devices may only read cards up to a certain size.
- It is small and easy to lose once it is out of the device.

# Flash drives: storage you carry

A flash drive (also called a flash disk) plugs into a USB port.

**Best for:**

- Moving documents between a home computer, an office, a school or a printing shop.
- Playing films on a TV or music in a car stereo with a USB port.
- Backups you keep in a drawer, away from the computer.
- Handing files to someone who has no internet or slow internet.

**Keep in mind:**

- Most flash drives have a USB-A plug, which fits computers, TVs and car stereos. To use one with a phone you usually need a USB OTG adapter that matches your phone's port, and a phone that supports OTG.
- They are easy to lose; a key-ring loop helps.

# Quick guide by task

- **My phone is full:** memory card, if your phone has a slot.
- **I print documents at a shop in town:** flash drive.
- **I want films on the TV:** flash drive, if the TV has a USB port.
- **Music in the car:** flash drive for a stereo with USB; memory card if the stereo has a card slot.
- **CCTV or dashcam:** memory card.
- **Backup of phone photos:** either, but a flash drive kept away from the phone is a separate copy if the phone is lost.

# Speed and size

Both come in many sizes. For a memory card, the speed class matters if you record video; for phones, Class 10 is the usual choice. For a flash drive, a USB 3.0 drive is faster in a USB 3.0 port and still works in older USB 2.0 ports, just at USB 2.0 speed.

Every memory card we sell is written full and read back before it is sold, so the size on the box is the size you get.

# When to have both

Many people use both: a memory card in the phone for everyday photos, and a flash drive to copy the best ones onto a computer or keep as a backup. A small business might keep CCTV on memory cards and its documents backed up on flash drives.
