No, fast charging does not damage your battery in any way you would notice, as long as your phone supports it and you use a proper charger and cable. The phone decides how fast to charge, and it slows down as the battery fills and if it gets hot. The thing that does age a battery faster is heat, so the habits around fast charging matter more than the charger itself.

# How fast charging works

A charger offers one or more output profiles, for example 5V⎓2A or 9V⎓2.22A. Volts × amps = watts, so those are 10W and about 20W.

Fast charging happens when the phone and the charger agree on a higher-power profile. They need a shared standard:

- **USB Power Delivery (PD)** over USB-C, used by iPhones from the iPhone 8 onwards and by many Android phones.
- **Quick Charge (QC)**, common on Android phones, often over USB-A.
- **Brand-specific standards** that some phone makers use with their own chargers.

If the phone and charger do not share a standard, the phone charges at normal speed. It does not take more than it can handle.

# Why it does not hurt the battery the way people fear

## The phone is in charge

The phone's charging circuit, not the charger, decides how much power the battery takes. It reads the battery's level and temperature and adjusts constantly.

## It is fast at the start, slow at the end

Most phones charge fastest when the battery is low, then slow down as it fills. The last part of a charge is gentle whatever charger you use. That is why phones advertise how fast they get to 50%, not to 100%.

## It backs off when it is hot

Most phones reduce the charging speed when the battery gets warm. If your phone fast charges in the morning but charges slowly on a hot afternoon, this is likely why.

# Where the real risk is: heat

Fast charging makes more heat than slow charging. Heat is what ages lithium-ion batteries fastest. So the question is not whether to fast charge, but how to keep it cool while you do.

- **Do not fast charge under a pillow** or on a bed. Use a table.
- **Take off a thick case** if the phone gets warm.
- **Do not game or video call** while fast charging.
- **Keep it out of the sun**, including on a car dashboard.
- **Use a slower charger overnight** if you like. When time is not a factor, there is no need for speed.

# Should you turn fast charging off?

Some phones let you switch fast charging off under Settings, then Battery. You might do this overnight or when you are not in a hurry. It is a small benefit, not a necessity. If you rely on quick top-ups between power cuts or before a long boda trip, keep it on.

# When the battery is the problem

If your phone gets hot with every charge, even on a good charger, cable and surface, the battery may be worn. If the back is lifting or the screen is pushing out, the battery is swollen: stop charging it and have it replaced.
