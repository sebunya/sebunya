A USB sound card is a small adapter that plugs into a computer's USB port and gives it its own headphone and microphone jacks. You need one when your computer's audio jack is broken, missing, crackly or in an awkward place, or when you simply want to plug a headset in at the front of a desktop.

# What it does

Every computer that plays sound has a "sound card" inside, usually a chip on the main board, connected to the headphone and mic sockets. A USB sound card does the same job outside the computer.

It takes the sound from the computer over USB, turns it into a signal your headphones can play, and does the reverse for your microphone. The computer treats it as a separate sound device.

# When it helps

**A broken or loose headphone jack.** Jacks wear out, especially on laptops that move around a lot. If sound crackles, cuts out or only plays on one side through the jack but your headphones work elsewhere, a USB sound card is a cheap workaround instead of a repair.

**A computer with no headphone jack.** Some computers have none, or have only one socket you would rather keep free.

**Awkward socket placement.** Many desktop computers have their audio sockets at the back. A USB sound card in a front USB port, or on a short USB extension, brings your headset within reach.

**Separate audio for calls.** Some people keep a USB sound card just for their call headset, so they can switch between the computer's speakers and the headset in the sound settings.

# What to check before you buy

- **A free USB-A port.** The ADT-S005 has a USB-A connector, the full-size rectangular plug. If your computer only has USB-C ports, you will need a suitable adapter.
- **Your headset's plug.** Some headsets have one 3.5mm plug for both sound and mic; others have two separate plugs. Separate headphone and mic jacks suit two-plug headsets directly. A single-plug headset may need a splitter to use both its sound and its mic. If you are unsure, bring or photograph your headset's plug and ask us.
- **What you want it to fix.** A USB sound card gives you working sockets. It is not a promise of louder or better sound than a working built-in jack.

# Setting it up

1. Plug the sound card into a USB port.
2. Plug your headphones and microphone into the matching jacks. They are usually marked with a headphone and a mic symbol, or by colour.
3. Open your computer's sound settings and choose the USB sound device for **output** (playback) and **input** (recording).
4. Play a sound and say something into the mic to test.

Many computers recognise USB audio devices on their own. If yours does not, check the instructions that come with the sound card, or ask us.

# If there is still no sound

- Check the USB device is selected for both output and input in the sound settings.
- Try a different USB port.
- Make sure the plugs are pushed fully into the right jacks.
- Test your headphones on a phone to rule them out. Wired earphones like our [GP-H06](/products/goldplus-earphones-gp-h06-type-pin) use a standard 3.5mm plug.
