The best time to prepare for a power cut is while the power is on. Keep at least one power bank charged and ready, top up everything whenever there is power, and make your phone battery stretch once it goes off. Here is a plan that works whether the outage lasts an hour or a whole evening.

# Before the power goes

**Keep a power bank full, not half empty.** A power bank sitting at 20% in a drawer is no help at 8pm. Recharge it after every outage, as soon as the power returns.

**Know how much is left.** A power bank with a digital charge display shows its level at a glance, so you are not guessing. **Choose the right size for your home.** If outages in your area last for hours, or more than one person depends on the same power bank, a 20,000mAh size makes more sense than a pocket 10,000mAh.

**Keep the cables with it.** A charged power bank and a missing cable is a common evening problem. Power banks with built-in cables solve it, as long as the connectors match your phones.

# When the power goes off

1. **Lower your screen brightness.** The screen is usually the biggest drain on a phone.
2. **Turn on battery saver.** Most phones have it in Settings, under Battery.
3. **Close apps you are not using,** especially video and games.
4. **Turn off what you do not need,** such as Bluetooth and location, if you are not using them.
5. **Charge in short top-ups.** Plug in when your phone drops low rather than keeping it on the power bank all evening. This leaves charge for others in the house.

# Light without the torch draining your phone

The phone torch is handy, but it uses the same battery you need for calls. A power bank with a built-in lamp lets you light the room and keep the phone for communication. Remember the lamp draws on the same battery that charges your phone, so watch the display and switch it off when you do not need it.

# Share fairly in a household

When several phones depend on one power bank, decide who comes first. Phones needed for work, school or calls to family usually matter most. Top each one up to a useful level rather than filling one phone completely while the others run flat.

# When the power comes back

- Recharge the power bank first, then the phones, if you have only one socket free.
- Unplug once both are full, rather than leaving everything on charge all night.
- Check the power bank does not feel unusually hot while it charges. A little warmth is normal; real heat, a bulge or a burning smell means stop using it.

# Safety during outages

- Never charge a power bank on a bed, under a pillow or covered with cloth. Charge it on a hard, open surface.
- Do not use a power bank that is swollen, cracked or wet.
- Keep candles and paraffin lamps away from power banks and phones.
