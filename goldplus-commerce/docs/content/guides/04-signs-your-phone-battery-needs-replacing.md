A phone battery usually needs replacing when the phone no longer lasts the day with your normal use, switches off while it still shows charge, or gets hot or bulges. Every lithium-ion battery wears with use and age, so these signs are normal for an older phone. The trick is telling a tired battery apart from a settings or app problem.

# The clear signs

- **It no longer lasts the day.** You used to get home at 7pm with charge left; now you are hunting for a socket by lunch, with the same apps and the same habits.
- **It switches off at 20% or 30%.** The phone dies suddenly, then shows a much higher figure when you plug it back in. A worn battery cannot hold its voltage when the phone asks for a burst of power.
- **The percentage jumps around.** It drops from 60% to 35% in a few minutes, or sits at 100% for an hour and then falls fast.
- **It only works on the charger.** If the phone restarts as soon as you unplug it, the battery is close to the end.
- **It charges slowly or stops short of 100%.** Before blaming the battery, try another cable and charger, because a worn cable causes the same symptom.

# The urgent sign: swelling

If the back cover lifts, the screen pushes out at one edge, or the phone no longer lies flat on a table, the battery may be swollen. A swollen lithium battery is a fire risk.

> Stop using and charging the phone. Do not press on it, try to flatten it, or open a sealed phone yourself. Keep it away from heat and from anything that burns, and have it looked at.

# Heat that is not normal

A phone gets warm while it charges, plays video or uses mobile data on a weak signal. That is normal. A phone that is too hot to hold comfortably, gets hot while sitting idle in your pocket, or smells of burning is not normal. Unplug it and let it cool somewhere safe.

# Battery or settings? A quick check

Before you buy anything, rule out the easy causes:

1. **Look at the battery usage screen.** On Android, open Settings, then Battery. On iPhone, open Settings, then Battery. If one app is using a large share, the problem may be that app, not the battery.
2. **Restart the phone.** A stuck app or a background process can drain a healthy battery.
3. **Check for updates.** A new update can cause heavy drain for a day or two while the phone re-indexes. If it goes on longer, look again.
4. **Think about signal.** A phone on a weak signal works harder to stay connected. If your drain is worst in one place, such as a village upcountry or a basement office, signal is part of the story.

If the drain stays heavy after all that, and the phone is more than a couple of years old, the battery is the likely cause.

## On an iPhone

iPhones show a Maximum Capacity figure under Settings, then Battery, then Battery Health (the menu name varies slightly by iOS version). A low figure, or a message that the battery needs service, confirms what you are feeling.

# What to do next

- **Find your exact model number** in Settings, then About phone. The name on the box is not precise enough.
- **Find the battery code** if your phone has a removable back. It is printed on the battery label, for example BL-49FT.
- **Match the code, not the shape or the mAh.** A battery that looks the same but has a different code is not a safe swap.
