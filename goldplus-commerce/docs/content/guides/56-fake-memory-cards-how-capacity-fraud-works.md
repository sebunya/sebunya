A fake memory card is usually a small card made to report a much bigger size. A card sold as 128GB might really hold only a fraction of that. It looks normal, shows the full size in your phone, and accepts files. Once the real space runs out, new files are lost or overwrite old ones, and you often do not find out until the photos you need will not open.

# How the trick works

Every memory card has a small controller chip that tells the phone or computer how much space there is. On a fake card, that controller is set to report a bigger number than the memory inside can hold.

- **Your phone believes the label.** It shows the full size and lets you keep saving.
- **The first part of the card works fine,** because there is real memory there.
- **Past the real capacity,** files have nowhere to go. Depending on the card, they are silently thrown away, or they write over files saved earlier.

That is why a fake card can seem perfect for weeks. The problem only appears once you have saved more than the real capacity.

# Warning signs

- Photos or videos that open as grey blocks, partly broken images, or not at all.
- Files that were there yesterday and are gone today.
- Folders that show the right file names, but the files are empty or corrupt.
- A camera or dashcam that records, but the footage stops partway or will not play.
- A price far below what the same size normally costs.
- Packaging with spelling mistakes, blurry printing, or no security marking.

None of these on their own proves a card is fake, but together they are a strong hint.

# How to test a card

The reliable test is the one that catches the trick: **fill the whole card with test data, then read every bit of it back** and check it matches.

- On Windows, a free tool such as **H2testw** does this.
- On Mac or Linux, a free tool such as **F3** does the same job.

Test a new card before you put anything important on it. Testing a large card can take a long time, so start it and leave it running. Testing erases nothing on a blank card, but back up any files already on a card before you test it.

Checking the size in your phone's settings is **not** a test. A fake card reports the false size there too.

> If a card fails the test, stop using it for anything important. Copy off whatever still opens, and do not trust it for photos, CCTV or backups.

# How to avoid fakes

- **Buy from a seller you can go back to,** with a shop address and a returns policy.
- **Be wary of prices that seem too good,** especially for very large cards.
- **Test new cards** before trusting them with photos you cannot replace.
- **Check for a verification code** where the brand offers one.
