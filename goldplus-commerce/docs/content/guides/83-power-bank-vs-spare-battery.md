For most people today, a power bank is the better choice. It works with almost any phone, charges other people's phones and earbuds too, and needs no tools. A spare battery only makes sense if your phone has a removable back cover and you want an instant full swap. If your phone dies early because its battery is worn out, neither is the real fix: replace the battery.

# How a spare battery works

On phones with a removable back, you can carry a second charged battery and swap it in when the first runs out. It is quick: switch off, open the back, swap, switch on. You go from empty to full in a minute, with no cable and no waiting.

The catch is that most phones now have sealed bodies. On those, the battery cannot be swapped by the user, and a spare battery is no use on the road.

# How a power bank works

A power bank is a separate battery with USB ports or built-in cables. You plug your phone in and it charges, either at standard speed or faster if the power bank, cable and phone support the same fast charging standard.

It takes longer than a battery swap, but you can keep using the phone while it charges.

# Side by side

**Compatibility.** A spare battery fits one phone model, or a small family of models. A power bank charges almost any phone, as long as you have the right connector.

**Speed.** A swap is instant. A power bank takes time, faster with fast charging.

**Sharing.** A spare battery helps only you. A power bank can help family, friends and colleagues, and charge earbuds too.

**Charging the spare.** A spare battery usually needs to be charged inside the phone, or in a separate charger. A power bank charges from any suitable USB charger.

**Size.** A spare battery is small and flat. A power bank is bigger, more so at 20,000mAh.

# Safety for both

Both contain lithium-ion cells, so the same rules apply:

- Never carry a loose spare battery in a pocket with keys or coins. Metal touching the contacts can short it. Keep it in a case.
- Never use a swollen battery or power bank. A bulge is a fire risk: stop using and charging it.
- Keep both out of heat and direct sun.
- On a plane, spare batteries and power banks go in cabin baggage, not checked luggage.

# When a replacement battery is the answer

If your phone used to last all day and now dies by early afternoon, switches off at 20% or 30%, or the back cover bulges, the battery itself is worn. Carrying extra power only works around the problem. A new battery restores the phone.

# Why not both?

Many people with removable-battery phones keep a spare battery for emergencies and a power bank for everyday top-ups and sharing. With a sealed phone, a power bank is the practical choice, and a fresh battery when the old one wears out.
