Store a spare phone battery about half charged, in a cool, dry place, with its metal contacts covered so nothing can touch them. Check it every few months and top it back up to around half. Keep it away from coins, keys and anything else metal, and never store a battery that is swollen or damaged.

# Why half charged

Lithium-ion batteries age fastest when they sit full and warm. Stored empty, they can drain so low over time that they are damaged and may not charge again. Around half charged is the comfortable middle.

If your spare goes into a phone to check its level, charge or use it until the phone shows roughly 40% to 60%, then take it out.

# Where to keep it

- **Cool:** room temperature or a bit below. Not on a windowsill, not near a cooker, not in a car.
- **Dry:** away from damp walls, bathrooms and sinks.
- **Out of reach:** away from children and pets.
- **On its own:** in a small box, its original packaging, or a plastic bag, not loose in a drawer full of odds and ends.

Do not keep batteries in a fridge or freezer. Moisture can form when they warm up.

# Cover the contacts

The metal contacts on a phone battery can short-circuit if something metal touches them both. That can make the battery very hot or start a fire.

- Put a piece of tape over the contacts, or keep the battery in its original cover.
- Never carry a loose battery in a pocket or bag with coins, keys or a pen.

# Check it every few months

Even unused, a battery slowly loses charge. Every three months or so:

1. Look at it. If it is puffy, bent or leaking, stop. Do not charge or use it.
2. Put it in the phone and check the level.
3. Top it up to around half if it has dropped.

# Carrying it day to day

A spare battery is handy for phones with removable backs, especially on long trips upcountry where sockets are scarce.

- Carry it in a small case or a bag of its own, contacts covered.
- Keep it out of direct sun, including on the seat of a matatu or in a boda passenger's pocket on a hot afternoon.
- If the battery gets hot, dented or wet, stop using it.

# Flying with a spare battery

Spare lithium batteries go in cabin baggage, not in checked bags. Airline limits are written in watt-hours. Phone batteries are usually far below the common limit of 100Wh for spare batteries without airline approval; batteries between 100Wh and 160Wh need the airline's approval. To work out Wh, multiply the mAh by the nominal voltage and divide by 1,000. Check your airline's current rules before you travel, and keep each spare in its own case or bag with the contacts covered.

# Is a power bank better?

For many people, yes. A spare battery only helps if your phone has a removable back and you swap it often. # When a spare is past it

If a stored battery is swollen, leaking, dented or will not hold a charge, do not use it. Tape the contacts and take it to a phone repair shop or an electronics recycler that accepts batteries. Do not put it in household rubbish or a fire.
