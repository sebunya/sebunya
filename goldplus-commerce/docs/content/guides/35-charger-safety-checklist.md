Most charging problems come from a damaged cable, an overloaded socket or a phone charging in the wrong place. This checklist covers the basics for a home, a shop or an office. Go through it once, then glance over it every few months.

# The charger

- **It is genuine and has a clear label.** The label should show the input (for example 100-240V ~50/60Hz) and each output in volts and amps. For GoldPlus products, check the code on the security hologram on our [verification page](/verification).
- **The case is undamaged.** No cracks, no melted spots, no scorch marks.
- **The pins are solid.** They should not wobble, bend or look discoloured.
- **It fits the socket firmly.** A charger that falls out or hangs loose makes a poor connection, which can heat up.
- **It stays warm, not hot.** Warm under load is normal. Too hot to hold is not.

# The cable

- **No fraying, splits or exposed wire.** Check both ends, where the cable bends most.
- **The plugs are firm.** A plug that wobbles in the phone or charger can cause a poor connection.
- **No tape repairs.** Tape hides damage but does not fix it. Replace the cable.
- **It is long enough.** A cable stretched tight from socket to bed gets pulled and damaged. A longer cable, such as our 2 m braided [GP-L12 USB-C to USB-C cable](/products/goldplus-cable-gp-l12), avoids that.
- **It is the right type.** For fast charging, the cable must match the charger's port and the phone's port.

# The socket and extension

- **Do not overload.** One extension feeding many chargers plus a kettle or iron is asking for trouble. Keep heavy appliances on their own sockets.
- **Do not chain extensions** or stack adapters.
- **Use surge protection** where power cuts and surges are common.
- **Keep extensions off the floor** in places that get mopped or where water can splash.
- **Replace scorched or loose sockets.** Have a qualified electrician look at any socket that sparks, crackles or feels hot.

# Where you charge

- **On a hard, flat surface.** Not on a bed, sofa or under a pillow, where heat builds up.
- **Away from water.** Not next to a sink, a kettle or a window that leaks in the rain.
- **Out of direct sun.** Phones slow down or stop charging when hot, and heat wears the battery.
- **Away from paper and fabric.** Keep chargers clear of curtains, papers and clothing.
- **Out of reach of small children.** And never let children chew on cables.

# In the office or shop

- **Label shared chargers.** It stops people grabbing unknown ones from home.
- **One charging point, set up properly.** A good extension on a desk is safer than cables trailing across the floor where people trip.
- **Unplug at the end of the day,** especially before a weekend or when power is unreliable.
- **Retire damaged chargers.** Put them in a box for disposal, not back in the drawer.

# Warning signs: stop and unplug

- A burning or plastic smell.
- Buzzing or crackling from the charger.
- Scorch marks on the charger, plug or socket.
- The phone connecting and disconnecting again and again.
- A phone that bulges or whose screen lifts at the edge. This is a swollen battery and a fire risk: stop using and charging it, keep it away from heat, and have it looked at.
