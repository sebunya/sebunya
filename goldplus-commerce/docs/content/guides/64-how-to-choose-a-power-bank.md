The right power bank is the one that matches how you use your phone, not the one with the biggest number on the box. Work through six questions: how much energy you need, how fast you want to charge, which connectors you use, which extras help, how much size you will carry, and whether the unit is genuine.

# 1. How much energy do you need?

Capacity is printed in mAh, but mAh on its own does not tell you how much energy is inside. Where the label prints watt-hours (Wh), compare those instead. Two labels with the same mAh can hold different amounts of energy if they are stated at different voltages.

As a rough guide to the choice:

- **About 10,000mAh** suits a day out with one phone and a pocket or small bag.
- **About 20,000mAh** suits long days, power cuts, travel, or more than one phone, and it is bigger and heavier.

No capacity figure promises an exact number of phone charges. Some energy is always lost in conversion, so your phone receives less than the label says.

# 2. How fast does it need to charge?

Speed is set by watts, not capacity. Read the output line on the label: volts multiplied by amps gives watts. 5V⎓2A is 10W, a steady standard charge. Higher figures such as 18W, 20W or 22.5W mean fast charging, but only when the phone, the power bank and the cable all support the same standard, such as USB Power Delivery (PD) over USB-C.

If your phone does not fast charge, a fast power bank still works safely; it just charges at your phone's normal speed.

# 3. Which connectors do you use?

Check the port on every device you plan to charge:

- **USB-C** on most newer Android phones and the latest iPhones.
- **Lightning** on older iPhones.
- **Micro-USB (often called V8)** on many older and budget phones.

A power bank with built-in cables saves you from forgetting a lead. Make sure the built-in connectors match your phones, or that you have the right [charging cable](/power/charging-cables) for the ports.

# 4. Which extras will you actually use?

Extras are useful when they fit your day, and dead weight when they do not.

- **Digital display:** shows how much charge is left, so you know when to top it up.
- **Wireless or magnetic charging:** handy if your phone supports it. Check your phone first.
- **Lamp:** a real help during power cuts and evenings without light.
- **Phone stand or lanyard:** small comforts for watching videos or carrying.

# 5. How much size will you carry?

A power bank left at home charges nothing. If you carry only a pocket, a compact 10,000mAh unit is the honest choice. If it lives in a bag or on a desk during outages, a larger one makes sense.

# 6. Is it genuine?

Fake power banks often claim capacities their size cannot hold. Every genuine GoldPlus product has a code on the security hologram on the packaging. Enter it on the [verification page](/verification) before you rely on the product. If a code is rejected, [report it](/support/fake).
