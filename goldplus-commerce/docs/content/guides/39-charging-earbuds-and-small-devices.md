Small devices such as wireless earbuds, smartwatches, fitness bands and Bluetooth speakers are easy to charge. They have small batteries and draw only a little power, so any well-made USB charger will do, including a phone fast charger. What matters more is the right cable or cradle, clean contacts and a sensible place to charge.

# Will a fast charger harm my earbuds?

No. Small devices charge at the basic 5V level. A fast charger only raises its voltage when a device asks for it using a standard like USB PD or Quick Charge. Earbuds and most watches do not ask, so the charger stays at 5V and the device draws the small current it needs.

If a device's manual recommends a particular charger or warns against certain ones, follow the manual. That is rare, but it does happen.

# Earbuds: the case does the work

With wireless earbuds, you charge the case, and the case charges the buds.

- **Plug the case in** with the right cable. Many cases use USB-C; some older or budget cases use Micro-USB. Check the port before buying a spare cable.
- **Seat the buds properly.** They sit on small metal contacts. If a bud does not charge, take it out, check for wax or dust, and put it back firmly.
- **Close the lid.** Some cases only charge the buds, or show the right light, with the lid closed.
- **Watch the case light.** Most cases show a light while charging and change it when full. The manual explains what each colour means.

# Smartwatches and fitness bands

Most watches and bands charge through a magnetic cradle or a clip that touches small contacts on the back.

- **Use the cradle made for your device.** Cradles look similar but are often not interchangeable.
- **Line it up properly.** If the watch does not show it is charging, re-seat it.
- **Keep the contacts clean.** Sweat and dirt build up on the back of a watch. Wipe the contacts with a dry, soft cloth.
- **Dry it first.** If you wore it in the rain or while exercising, let it dry before charging.

# Keep contacts clean and dry

Moisture and dirt on charging contacts can stop charging and, over time, cause corrosion.

- Wipe contacts with a dry cloth or a cotton bud.
- Never charge a device that is wet.
- Do not use metal tools to clean contacts or ports.

# Safety for small batteries

- Charge on a hard surface, away from bedding.
- Do not leave earbuds or watches charging in a hot car or in direct sun.
- If a case or watch swells, gets very hot or smells, stop charging it. A swollen battery is a fire risk.
- Do not charge a device that has been crushed or dropped in water.

# Store them well

If you will not use earbuds for a while, charge them part way rather than leaving them empty, and store them somewhere cool and dry.
