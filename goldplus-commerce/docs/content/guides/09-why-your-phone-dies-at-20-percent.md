When a phone switches off at 20% or 30%, the battery is usually worn. An old battery cannot hold its voltage steady when the phone suddenly asks for more power, for example opening the camera or starting a call. The voltage dips, the phone protects itself by shutting down, and the percentage it showed turns out to have been optimistic.

"Calibrating" the battery can sometimes make the percentage more accurate. It cannot make an old battery hold more charge.

# How a phone guesses its percentage

The phone does not see a fuel tank. It estimates how much charge is left from the battery's voltage, how much current is flowing, and what it has learned about the battery over time. Then it turns that into a percentage.

On a healthy battery the estimate is good. On a worn battery, two things go wrong:

- **It holds less than the phone expects,** so the percentage falls faster than it should.
- **Its voltage sags under load.** At 20% on an old battery, a burst of activity can push the voltage below the level the phone needs to stay on. The phone switches off to protect the battery and your data.

When you plug it back in, the voltage recovers and the phone may show 15% or 25%. The charge was there; the battery just could not deliver it fast enough.

# What calibration does, and does not do

Calibration means letting the phone see the battery's full range so it can correct its estimate. People usually do it by charging to 100%, using the phone until it switches off, and then charging uninterrupted back to 100%.

What it can do:

- Fix a percentage that jumps or sticks, for example one that sits at 100% for an hour and then falls fast.
- Help after a battery replacement, when the phone is still learning the new battery.

What it cannot do:

- Restore capacity a worn battery has lost.
- Stop voltage sag in an old battery.
- Fix a swollen or damaged battery.

Running a battery completely flat is hard on it, so do this rarely, not as a weekly habit. Many modern phones adjust their estimate on their own over normal use.

# Other things that cause early shutdowns

- **Cold.** Lithium batteries deliver less power when cold. This is rare in Kampala, but it can happen on a high-altitude trip or in a very cold air-conditioned room.
- **Heat.** A very hot phone may shut down to protect itself. Let it cool.
- **Software.** An app stuck in the background or a faulty update can cause odd behaviour. Restart the phone and check for updates.
- **A failing charger port or cable** can make it look as if the battery is not charging fully.

## On an iPhone

Under Settings, then Battery, then Battery Health, iPhones show the battery's Maximum Capacity and whether the phone is limiting peak performance to prevent unexpected shutdowns. If it says shutdowns have happened, the battery is the cause.

# What to do

1. **Restart the phone** and check for software updates.
2. **Check the battery usage screen** under Settings, then Battery, for an app using far more than its share.
3. **Try one calibration cycle** if the percentage behaves strangely.
4. **If it still dies at 20%, replace the battery.** That is the real fix.
