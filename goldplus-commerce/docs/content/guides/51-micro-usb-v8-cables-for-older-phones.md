Micro-USB, which most people in Uganda call **V8**, is still on many older and budget phones, feature phones, Bluetooth speakers, MiFis, radios and torches. A good V8 cable keeps all of them going. Choose one with the length you need, a solid rating, and plugs built to survive daily plugging in.

# How to recognise Micro-USB

- The plug is small and shaped like a squashed trapezium: one long side is wider than the other.
- It fits **only one way up.** If it does not slide in, turn it over. Forcing it is the fastest way to damage the port.
- It is smaller and flatter than the old Mini-USB found on some very old gadgets, and it is not reversible like USB-C.

The other end of a V8 cable is usually **USB-A**, the large rectangular plug that fits most chargers, computers, car chargers and power banks.

# Why V8 cables wear out

Micro-USB plugs only go in one way and have small, thin metal parts. Many owners plug and unplug them several times a day, often in the dark, and the plug works loose over time. Phones with V8 ports also tend to be the ones that are a few years old, so the port may already be worn.

That makes the cable's build worth paying attention to:

- **Reinforced plugs** with a strain-relief collar last longer at the joint.
- **A braided jacket** resists kinks and scuffs.
- **A thick copper core** keeps voltage loss low, especially on a long cable.

# Choosing length and rating

- **1 m** for charging at a desk, in a bag, or from a power bank.
- **2 m** for charging in bed or across a room.
- **The amp rating** is the most current the cable is built to carry. A higher rating does not force more power into the phone; it just stops the cable being the limit. Your phone and charger decide the actual speed.

# Keeping a worn port charging

If the phone only charges when the cable is held at an angle:

1. Switch the phone off.
2. Look into the port with a torch. Lint from pockets often packs in and stops the plug seating fully.
3. Gently clear it with a wooden toothpick. Never use metal or water.
4. Try a different cable. If a new cable works, the old one was the problem.
5. If nothing helps, the port itself may need repair.

# One cable for many gadgets

Because so many small devices still use V8, a spare cable is handy to keep:

- In the car, for a passenger's phone or a speaker.
- With your MiFi or radio, so it is never without its charger.
- In a bag for upcountry travel, where you may borrow someone else's charger plug.
