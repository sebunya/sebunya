Replacing an iPhone battery is a common repair, and a new battery can make an older iPhone feel like it did when it was new. Before you do it, check three things: the exact model of your iPhone, that your data is backed up, and who will fit the battery. iPhones are sealed and glued, so fitting a battery is a job for someone with the right tools and experience.

# Step 1: know your exact model

iPhone batteries are made for one model. An iPhone 13 battery does not fit an iPhone 13 Pro, and an iPhone 11 Pro Max battery does not fit an iPhone 11.

To find your model:

- Open **Settings**, then **General**, then **About**. The model name is listed there.
- Tapping the model number shows a code starting with A, which identifies the exact version.

The battery you buy should name your exact model.

# Step 2: check whether you really need it

Open **Settings**, then **Battery**, then **Battery Health**. If Maximum Capacity is low, or the screen says performance management has been applied or a service is recommended, a new battery should help.

If the battery health looks fine but the phone drains fast, check the battery usage list for an app using far more than its share, and restart the phone before buying anything.

# Step 3: back up first

Any repair that opens the phone carries a small risk. Back up to iCloud or a computer before you hand it over. Turn off Find My only if the technician asks you to, and turn it back on afterwards.

# What changes after a replacement

## A notice in Settings

On many recent iPhones, iOS checks whether the battery is a genuine Apple part fitted through Apple's own repair process. If it cannot confirm that, you may see a message in Settings saying it cannot verify the battery, or the battery listed as an unknown part. On some models, Battery Health may also stop showing a Maximum Capacity figure.

The phone still works and charges normally when this happens. It is worth knowing in advance so the notice does not surprise you. Ask whoever fits the battery what to expect on your model.

## Water resistance

iPhones are sealed with adhesive to resist water. Opening the phone breaks that seal. A careful repair replaces the adhesive, but treat the phone as less water-resistant than before. Keep it away from water, and do not rely on it surviving rain on a boda ride.

## The first few days

The phone needs a few charge cycles to learn the new battery. The percentage may look slightly off at first. Use it normally and it settles.

# Who should fit it

- **Apple or an authorised service provider** fits Apple batteries and calibrates them as part of the repair.
- **An independent technician** can fit a replacement battery. Choose someone who opens iPhones regularly, uses proper tools, and replaces the adhesive.
- **Doing it yourself** is possible with the right kit and patience, but the screen cables are fragile and the old battery is glued in. A tool slipping into a battery can start a fire. If you have not done it before, let a technician do it.

Never try to remove or replace a swollen battery yourself. Stop using and charging the phone and have a technician handle it.

# Look after the new battery

- Keep the phone out of hot cars and direct sun.
- Turn on Optimised Battery Charging, or a charging limit if your model has one, under Settings, then Battery.
- Charge with a labelled charger and a cable in good condition. For Lightning iPhones that support fast charging, a USB-C to Lightning cable such as the [GoldPlus GP-PD03](/products/goldplus-cable-gp-pd03l) works with a USB-C charger.
