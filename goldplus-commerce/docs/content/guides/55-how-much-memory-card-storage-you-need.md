For most people who mainly take photos and keep some music, **32GB or 64GB** is a comfortable card for a phone. If you film a lot of video, especially in 4K, look at **128GB or more**. CCTV and dashcams fill whatever you give them, so size there decides how many days of footage you keep.

The figures below are rough. Real file sizes vary a lot with the phone, the camera settings and what you are filming, so treat every number as approximate.

# Rough sizes of everyday files

## Photos

A typical phone photo is somewhere around **2 to 5 MB**. Photos from high-resolution modes, or in RAW, can be much larger.

As a very rough guide:

- **1GB** holds a few hundred phone photos.
- **32GB** holds several thousand.

## Video

Video uses space far faster than photos:

- **Full HD (1080p):** very roughly **100 to 200 MB per minute**, depending on frame rate and settings.
- **4K:** very roughly **300 to 500 MB per minute**, and more at higher frame rates.

So an hour of 4K video can use tens of gigabytes. That is why heavy video users fill a phone so quickly.

## Music

A typical downloaded song is roughly **3 to 10 MB**, depending on its quality. A 16GB card holds a few thousand songs, which is plenty for most car and radio collections.

## WhatsApp

WhatsApp photos, videos and voice notes build up quietly, especially from busy groups. Check how much space it uses in WhatsApp's settings under Storage and data; it may be more than you expect.

# A simple way to choose

1. **Look at what you have now.** Open Settings, then Storage, and see how much your photos, videos and media use.
2. **Allow for growth.** Pick a card at least double what you use today, so you do not fill it in a few months.
3. **Check your device's limit.** Older phones and radios may not read cards above 32GB. Your manual or settings will say.

# Size by use

- **Radio, MP3 player or an old phone:** 1GB to 8GB is usually enough, and older devices may only read small cards.
- **Phone with photos and music:** 32GB to 64GB.
- **Phone that films a lot, or 4K:** 128GB to 256GB.
- **Dashcam:** follow the dashcam's manual for the largest card it supports. Bigger cards hold more trips before old footage is recorded over.
- **CCTV:** a bigger card keeps more days of footage before it loops. 256GB or 512GB keeps the most.

> More space is not a backup. If a phone is lost or a card fails, everything on it goes too. Copy important photos to a computer, a flash drive or the cloud now and then.

# Why your card shows a bit less

Card makers count a gigabyte as 1,000,000,000 bytes, while phones often count in units of 1,024. So a 64GB card may show about 59GB free. That is normal. Every memory card we sell is written full and read back before it is sold, so the size on the box is the size you get.
