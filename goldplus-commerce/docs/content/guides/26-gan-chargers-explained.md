GaN stands for gallium nitride, a material used for some of the switching parts inside a charger. GaN parts can switch faster and waste less energy as heat than the silicon parts used in older chargers. Less heat and faster switching mean the other parts can be smaller, so a GaN charger can give the same power from a smaller, lighter body.

GaN does not make your phone charge faster on its own. The speed still depends on the charger's output, the fast-charging standard, the cable and the phone.

# What happens inside a charger

A wall charger takes mains electricity from the socket and turns it into the low voltage your phone uses. To do that efficiently, modern chargers switch the current on and off very quickly, many thousands of times a second, and pass it through a small transformer.

Two things limit how small a charger can be:

- **Heat.** Every switch wastes a little energy as heat. A charger has to be big enough to get rid of that heat safely.
- **The size of the transformer and other parts.** Faster switching lets these parts be smaller.

# Why gallium nitride helps

Gallium nitride handles high voltages well and switches faster with lower losses than silicon. In a charger that means:

- **Less wasted energy**, so less heat to manage.
- **Faster switching**, so smaller transformers and filters.
- **A smaller case** for the same power, or more power in the same size.

That is why GaN became popular for chargers with higher outputs, such as those that charge a laptop and a phone together. Without GaN, those chargers tended to be large bricks.

# What GaN does not change

It is easy to read too much into the word GaN. Keep these points in mind:

- **It is not a fast-charging standard.** GaN is about the parts inside. PD and Quick Charge are the languages the charger and phone use to agree on power. A GaN charger still needs to support the standard your phone uses.
- **It does not raise the output.** The output lines on the label tell you the power. A small GaN charger and a large silicon charger with the same label give the same power.
- **It still gets warm.** Any charger warms up under load. GaN chargers are small, so the warmth is spread over less surface. Warm is normal; too hot to hold is not.
- **Smaller does not mean safer or less safe.** Safety comes from good design, protection circuits and genuine parts, not the material alone.

# Reading a GaN charger's label

Read it the same way as any other charger:

- Find the **output lines for each port**. Volts times amps gives the power of each listed profile.
- If there are several ports, find the line for **all ports together**. The power is shared when more than one device is connected.
- Check which port supports which standard, for example PD on the USB-C port.

If the box and the label disagree, trust the label on the charger, and ask us to read the exact unit before you order.

# When a GaN charger makes sense

- **You travel or commute a lot.** A small charger fits in a bag or pocket on a boda trip or an upcountry bus.
- **You charge several devices from one socket.** Higher-power multi-port chargers are where GaN's size saving is most noticeable.
- **You want one charger for a phone and a laptop.** Check the laptop's needs first.

If you only charge one phone at normal speed, a GaN charger is a nice-to-have rather than a must.
