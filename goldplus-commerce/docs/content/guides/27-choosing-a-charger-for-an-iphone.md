For an iPhone, the charger that matters is a **USB-C charger with USB Power Delivery (PD)**, plus a cable with USB-C at the charger end. iPhones from the iPhone 8 onwards fast charge this way, and a charger that gives about 20W from its USB-C port is the usual choice. An older USB-A charger still works, just more slowly.

# Step 1: check your iPhone's port

iPhones use one of two ports:

- **Lightning:** the small flat connector used on many iPhones before the iPhone 15.
- **USB-C:** the oval connector used from the iPhone 15 onwards.

Look at the bottom of your phone, or check the model in Settings, General, About. This decides which cable you need.

# Step 2: pick the cable

- **Lightning iPhone:** a **USB-C to Lightning** cable. This is what lets a Lightning iPhone fast charge from a PD charger.
- **USB-C iPhone:** a **USB-C to USB-C** cable.

A USB-A to Lightning cable (the older kind with the rectangular plug) cannot use PD, because PD only works over USB-C at the charger end. Your iPhone will charge with it, but at normal speed.

# Step 3: pick the charger

What to look for:

- A **USB-C port** that supports **PD**.
- An output line for that port at **9V** with enough current for about 20W, for example 9V⎓2.22A.
- A UK three-pin plug, so it fits Ugandan sockets without an adapter.

A charger with more power than your iPhone needs is fine. The iPhone asks for what it can take and the charger gives only that. A higher-power charger is handy if you also charge an iPad or a laptop.

# What about the old 5W cube?

The small white 5W charger that came with older iPhones is safe, but slow. A PD charger with a USB-C to Lightning cable can take an older iPhone from low to around half charged in a much shorter time. If you charge in short bursts between power cuts, that difference matters.

# Checks before you buy

- **Read the label, not the box.** The output lines for the USB-C port are what count.
- **Check the cable's connectors at both ends.** USB-C to Lightning or USB-C to USB-C.
- **Check the cable's condition.** A frayed or loose cable charges slowly and can get hot.
- **Verify it is genuine.** Every genuine GoldPlus product has a code on the security hologram you can check on our [verification page](/verification).

# Charging habits that help an iPhone

- Charge on a hard surface, not under a pillow.
- Take thick cases off if the phone gets very warm while charging.
- iPhones slow down charging near full on purpose. That is normal, not a fault.
