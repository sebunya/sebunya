A good backup is simple: keep **three copies** of anything important, on **two different kinds of storage**, with **one copy kept somewhere else**. That is the 3-2-1 idea. For a small shop or a family, it can be as easy as a computer, a flash drive kept at another place, and a cloud backup.

# Why it matters

Files are lost in ordinary ways:

- A phone is stolen in town or dropped on a boda trip.
- A laptop stops starting, or its storage fails.
- A power cut hits during saving and a file is corrupted.
- A flash drive is lost, or a memory card fails.
- Water, fire or theft takes everything kept in one place.

A single copy, wherever it is, can be lost in a moment. Two copies in the same bag or the same office can be lost together.

# The 3-2-1 idea in plain terms

1. **Three copies.** The original plus two backups.
2. **Two kinds of storage.** For example, a computer and a flash drive, or a phone and the cloud. Different kinds rarely fail at the same time.
3. **One copy elsewhere.** At home if the business is in town, at the shop if the files are at home, or in the cloud.

# What to back up

**For a small business:**

- Sales records, invoices and receipts.
- Stock lists and price lists.
- Customer and supplier contacts.
- Tax and registration documents, and scanned agreements.
- Your logo, product photos and designs.

**For a family:**

- Photos and videos from phones.
- Scanned IDs, certificates and school reports.
- Important documents and contacts.

# A simple routine

1. **Pick a day.** Once a week suits most small businesses; once a month may be enough for a family.
2. **Copy to a flash drive.** Make a folder named with the date, and copy your important files into it.
3. **Use two drives in turn.** Back up to drive A one week and drive B the next, keeping one of them at another place. If one drive is lost or fails, the other is at most a week behind.
4. **Turn on cloud backup** on your phone or computer for photos and documents, if your data costs allow it.
5. **Check it works.** Now and then, open a few files from the backup to make sure they open.

# Keeping backups safe

- **Label each drive** clearly, and keep it in a safe, dry place away from heat.
- **Eject before unplugging** every time.
- **Protect sensitive files.** A flash drive is easy to lose. For private records, use password-protected files or your computer's own encryption, and keep the password somewhere safe.
- **Do not keep your only backup in the same bag** as the laptop or phone.
- **Replace old drives** that start giving errors.

> A memory card in your phone is extra space, not a backup. If the phone is lost, the card goes with it.

# How big a drive?

Check how much space your important files use, then pick a drive with room to spare. Documents and spreadsheets take little space; photos and videos take much more. A 64GB or 128GB drive suits most small-business records and a good part of a family's photos. A 256GB drive suits a whole laptop backup or years of video.
