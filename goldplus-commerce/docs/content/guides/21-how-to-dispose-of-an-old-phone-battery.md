The safe way to get rid of an old phone battery is: do not bin it, do not burn it. Cover its metal contacts with tape, keep it in a small box or bag on its own, and take it to a phone repair shop or an electronics recycler that accepts batteries. Ask them before you go.

# Why it matters

Phone batteries are lithium-ion. Even an "empty" one usually holds some charge. If it is crushed, punctured or short-circuited, it can get very hot or catch fire.

- **In a rubbish bin or a skip,** a battery can be crushed with other waste and start a fire.
- **In a fire,** a lithium battery can burst and burn violently, and gives off harmful fumes. Never burn batteries with rubbish.
- **Buried or dumped,** its metals and chemicals do not belong in soil or water.

# Step 1: make it safe

1. **Look at it.** Is it flat and undamaged, or swollen, dented or leaking? If it is swollen or damaged, handle it as little as possible and see the section below.
2. **Cover the contacts.** Put a piece of tape, such as insulating tape or clear tape, over the metal contacts so nothing can touch them.
3. **Bag it on its own.** A small plastic bag or a small cardboard box, one battery per bag if you have several.

# Step 2: store it until you can drop it off

- Somewhere cool and dry, away from sun and heat.
- Out of reach of children and pets.
- Away from coins, keys and other metal.
- Not in a drawer full of loose odds and ends.

Do not collect old batteries for months in a tin. Take them in reasonably soon.

# Step 3: take it somewhere that accepts batteries

- **A phone repair shop.** Many replace batteries every day and may take old ones. Ask first.
- **An electronics or e-waste recycler.** Look for one near you that takes batteries, and ask what they accept.
- **Where you have the battery replaced.** If a technician fits your new battery, ask them what they do with the old one.

If you are not sure who accepts batteries in your area, call or message before you travel, so you do not carry it around for nothing.

# If the battery is swollen or damaged

A swollen battery, one with a puffy pouch or that has pushed a phone's back off, is at higher risk of fire.

- **Do not press, flatten or puncture it.**
- **Do not try to take a swollen battery out of a sealed phone yourself.** Ask a technician.
- **Put it on a hard, non-flammable surface,** such as a tiled floor or a concrete step, away from anything that burns, while you arrange disposal.
- **Do not charge it** or put it back in a phone.
- **If it hisses, smokes or gets hot,** keep away from it and keep others away.

# What about the old phone?

If you are getting rid of the whole phone, the battery goes with it to the recycler or repair shop. Before you hand it over:

- Back up your photos and contacts.
- Sign out of your Google or Apple account.
- Reset the phone to factory settings.
- Take out the SIM card and any memory card.

# What not to do

- Do not throw batteries in household rubbish.
- Do not burn them with garden waste or rubbish.
- Do not leave loose batteries where children can find them.
- Do not post batteries in an envelope without checking the courier's rules.
