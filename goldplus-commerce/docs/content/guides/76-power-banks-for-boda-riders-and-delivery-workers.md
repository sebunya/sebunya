For boda riders and delivery workers, the phone is the job: ride apps, maps, calls from customers and mobile money all run on it. The right power bank lasts a full shift, has the cable built in so it cannot be forgotten, and is carried in a bag out of the sun and rain. A 20,000mAh size suits most full days on the road.

# Why riders run out of charge

Navigation with the screen on, ride and delivery apps tracking your location, and constant calls are some of the heaviest loads a phone carries. Add bright sunlight, which pushes screen brightness up, and a full battery can be gone by early afternoon.

# What to look for

**Enough for a full shift.** A 20,000mAh power bank gives far more room than a 10,000mAh one. If you work long days, the bigger size is worth it. No capacity promises a fixed number of phone charges, because energy is lost as heat along the way.

**Built-in cables.** A loose cable is easy to lose at a stage or leave at a customer's gate. Built-in cables travel with the power bank. Check the connectors match your phone.

**A charge display.** You can see at a glance whether you can take that long trip or need to top up first.

**Something to hold on to.** A lanyard loop or carry handle makes it easier to carry and harder to drop.

# Protect it on the road

**Sun.** Never leave the power bank on the seat, on the tank or in a phone holder in direct sun. Heat ages the cells and can be dangerous. Keep it in a bag or jacket pocket, in the shade.

**Rain.** Water and charging do not mix. Keep the power bank in a closed bag, ideally inside a plastic bag or pouch, during rain. Do not charge it or use it while it is wet.

**Dust.** Dusty roads fill ports and connectors. Keep the cables tucked away, and wipe the connectors with a dry cloth.

**Vibration and drops.** Secure it in a bag so it does not bounce around. After a hard drop, check for cracks or swelling.

# Charging while riding

If your phone sits in a handlebar holder, avoid a cable stretched tightly between the bag and the phone. A pulled cable damages the connector and can yank the phone. Better options:

1. Top up during stops, waiting for customers or at the stage.
2. Keep the cable slack and secured if you must charge while moving.
3. Lower screen brightness when you can and close apps you are not using.

# End of the day

Recharge the power bank every night while the power is on, on a hard surface, not on a mattress. Unplug once it is full. A power bank that starts every shift full is the one that saves your day.
