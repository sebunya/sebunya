On a dual-port charger, the big number is usually the most the charger can give in total, or from one port used alone. It is not what each port gives at the same time. When you connect two phones, the charger shares its power, and each port may fall back to a lower profile.

Two phones can charge from one socket perfectly well. You just need to read the label to know what to expect.

# What the label tells you

A dual-port charger's label normally has three kinds of output line:

- **The USB-C port alone:** for example, a list of profiles such as 5V, 9V and 12V with their currents.
- **The USB-A port alone:** often a different list, sometimes with Quick Charge.
- **Both ports together:** a combined line, sometimes written as "total output" or "C + A".

Multiply volts by amps for each line to get watts. **Do not add the single-port figures together.** The combined line is the figure that applies when both ports are in use.

# What happens when you plug in a second phone

Many dual-port chargers do something like this:

1. With one phone, the port it is on can use its full listed profile.
2. When a second phone is connected, the charger often resets both ports for a moment. You may see charging stop and restart on both phones. That is normal.
3. Both ports then run at lower profiles that fit within the combined limit. Fast charging may drop to normal speed on one or both ports.

The exact behaviour depends on the charger. The combined line on the label is the reliable guide.

# Where the power goes

The phone on each port still decides what it takes within that port's limit. So:

- An older phone that charges at 5V draws only a little, leaving more for the other port.
- Two fast-charging phones compete for the total, so both charge slower than they would alone.
- A phone that is nearly full draws less, and the other phone can benefit.

# How to get the best speed

- **Put the phone in a hurry on the port that suits it.** An iPhone or a PD Android phone should use the USB-C port with a USB-C cable.
- **Charge the urgent phone alone first.** If someone needs to leave soon, give them the charger for twenty minutes, then plug the second phone in.
- **Use good cables on both ports.** A weak cable on one port slows only that phone, but it is still worth replacing.
- **Do not judge by the box.** If the box says one number and the label another, trust the label. Ask us to read the exact unit if you are unsure.

# Is it safe to charge two phones at once?

Yes. A well-made multi-port charger manages its own total and protects itself if the load is too high. It will get warmer with two phones than with one, so keep it in open air, not behind a sofa or under bedding.
