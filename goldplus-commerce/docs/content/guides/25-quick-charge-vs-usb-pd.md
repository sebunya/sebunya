Quick Charge 3.0 (QC 3.0) and USB Power Delivery (PD) both make a phone charge faster by raising the voltage. The difference is who made them and how they connect. QC comes from Qualcomm and is mostly found on USB-A ports. PD is the USB standard for USB-C. iPhones use PD. Many Android phones use PD, QC, or both.

The simple rule: **fast charging only happens when the charger and the phone share a standard.** If they do not, the phone still charges safely at normal speed.

# What Quick Charge 3.0 is

Quick Charge is a fast-charging method made by Qualcomm, the company behind the Snapdragon chips in many Android phones. QC 3.0 lets the phone ask the charger for a voltage between about 3.6V and 12V, in small steps, so it can find an efficient level as it charges.

QC 3.0 chargers usually use a USB-A port, and they are commonly rated up to about 18W. You will often see the QC logo or the words "Quick Charge 3.0" on the charger or its box.

# What USB PD is

USB Power Delivery is the fast-charging standard written by the USB group for USB-C. The charger and the device agree on a voltage profile (such as 5V, 9V or higher) before more power flows. PD covers everything from phones to laptops.

A newer PD feature called **PPS** allows small voltage steps, similar in spirit to QC. Some Samsung phones use PD with PPS for their fastest charging.

# Which phones use which

- **iPhone:** PD. iPhones from the iPhone 8 onwards fast charge with a USB-C PD charger. They do not use Quick Charge.
- **Samsung:** Samsung's fast charging is built on PD (and PPS for the faster modes). Some older Samsung models also used Samsung's own Adaptive Fast Charging.
- **Phones with Snapdragon chips:** often support Quick Charge, and many newer ones support PD too.
- **Tecno, Infinix, itel and other brands:** these use a range of methods, sometimes their own. Check your exact model's specification page or the original charger's label rather than guessing.

Newer versions of Quick Charge (QC 4 and later) are designed to work with USB PD, which is one reason PD is now the more common choice for new phones.

# How to find out what your phone uses

1. **Look at the charger that came with it.** The output label lists the voltages it gives, and often a QC or PD mark.
2. **Check the phone's official spec page.** Search the exact model number from Settings, About phone. It usually says "PD", "QC" or the brand's own name for fast charging.
3. **Look at the phone's port.** A Micro-USB phone cannot use PD, because PD needs USB-C.

# Common mix-ups

- **"QC" on a USB-C phone does not guarantee QC over USB-C.** Check which port the QC label applies to.
- **A USB-A port cannot do PD.** Plugging an iPhone into a USB-A port with a USB-A to Lightning cable gives normal-speed charging.
