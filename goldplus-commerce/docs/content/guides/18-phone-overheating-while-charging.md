A phone getting warm while it charges is normal. Charging always makes some heat, and fast charging makes more, especially in the first part of the charge. It becomes a problem when the phone is too hot to hold comfortably, the plug or cable end is hot, you smell burning, or the phone's back starts to bulge. Those signs mean: unplug and investigate.

# What is normal

- **Warm to the touch**, especially near the camera or the middle of the back, where the battery and chip sit.
- **Warmer during fast charging** than with a slow charger.
- **Warmer if you use the phone** while it charges, particularly for video, games or calls.
- **Cooling down** once charging slows near full, or once you stop using it.

Many phones slow down charging on their own when they get warm, to protect the battery. If your phone suddenly charges slowly on a hot day, that may be why.

# What is not normal

Unplug straight away if:

- The phone is too hot to hold.
- The charging port, the plug or the cable end is hot.
- You smell burning plastic or a sweet chemical smell.
- You see sparks, scorch marks or melted plastic.
- The back cover lifts, the screen pushes out, or the phone no longer lies flat. That is a swollen battery. Do not charge it again.
- It gets hot while charging but is not being used and the room is cool.

# Common causes, and the fix

## Where it is charging

A phone on a bed, a pillow or a sofa cushion cannot shed heat. Charge on a hard table, out of the sun.

## A thick case

Rubber and leather cases hold heat in. Take the case off while charging if the phone gets warm.

## Using it heavily while charging

Gaming or video calls while fast charging heat the phone from two directions. Charge first and play after, or switch to a slower charger while you use it.

## The cable

A frayed, bent or loose cable has more resistance, and resistance makes heat. If the cable end gets hot or the connection drops when you move it, replace it. A cable that sits firmly in the port, such as the braided [GoldPlus GP-L14T](/products/goldplus-cable-gp-l14t) USB-C cable, is a cheap fix.

## The charging port

Lint and dust from pockets build up in the port and stop the plug sitting fully home. That can cause poor contact and heat. Switch the phone off and clean the port gently with a wooden toothpick. Never use a metal pin.

## The charger

A charger with no printed output, or one that gets very hot itself, is a risk. Use a charger with its output clearly marked, for example 5V⎓2A. The [GoldPlus GP-C08](/products/goldplus-charger-gp-c08) lists its output and circuit protection on its page; the [GoldPlus GP-C09](/products/goldplus-charger-gp-c09) is a USB-C PD charger that lists over-voltage and over-current protection.

A higher-rated charger is not a problem in itself. The phone only draws the power it can handle.

## The battery

An old battery can run hotter than a new one. If the phone gets hot with every charge, even with a good charger and cable, on a hard surface, without a case, the battery may be worn. Check battery health where your phone offers it: on iPhone, Settings, then Battery, then Battery Health.

# What to do right now if it is too hot

1. Unplug it from the wall, then from the phone.
2. Take off the case and close heavy apps.
3. Put it on a hard surface in the shade. Do not put it in a fridge or freezer.
4. Once it is cool, try a different cable and charger. If it heats up again, stop charging it and have it checked.
