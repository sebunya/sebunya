The habits that make a phone battery last longest are simple: keep it cool, top it up instead of running it flat, avoid leaving it at 100% while it is hot, and charge with a charger and cable you trust. You do not need to follow strict rules. A few small changes, done most of the time, are enough.

# 1. Keep it cool

Heat ages a lithium-ion battery faster than anything else. Charging adds heat, so the two together matter most.

- Charge on a table or a hard surface, not on a bed, under a pillow or on a sofa cushion.
- If the phone gets hot while charging, take off a thick case.
- Do not charge on a car dashboard in the sun, or leave the phone in a parked car.
- If the phone is already hot from a long call or a game, let it cool before you plug in.

# 2. Top up, do not run it flat

Lithium-ion batteries prefer shallow charges. Going from 40% to 80% is gentler than going from 0% to 100%.

- Plug in when it is convenient, even at 50% or 60%.
- Try not to let the phone switch itself off from an empty battery often.
- You do not need to charge to 100% every time. If you will be out all day, charge full; if not, 80% is fine.

There is no need to "train" a battery by running it flat. That advice is for much older battery types.

# 3. Do not leave it hot and full

A battery sitting at 100% for many hours ages a little faster, and more so if it is warm. Many phones now have a setting that slows the last part of charging or stops at a lower level.

- **iPhone:** look for Optimised Battery Charging, or a charging limit option, under Settings, then Battery.
- **Android:** look under Settings, then Battery for options such as adaptive charging or battery protection. The name and location vary by brand.

These settings are worth turning on if you charge overnight.

# 4. Use a charger with a clear label

A charger should have its output printed on it, for example 5V⎓2A. That tells you what it gives your phone. A charger with no markings, or one that gets very hot, is not worth the risk.

# 5. Look after the cable and the port

- Replace a cable that is frayed, kinked or loose in the port. A damaged cable can get hot and charge slowly.
- Check the charging port for lint and dust, especially if the phone lives in a pocket. Clean it gently with a wooden toothpick, never a metal pin.
- Plug in fully. A connector that only half sits in the port can spark and heat up.

# 6. Plan for power cuts and long days

When the power goes off in the evening, the temptation is to run the phone to 0% and then charge it to 100% the moment power returns. A power bank lets you keep topping up instead. # What you can stop worrying about

- **Charging overnight** is fine on a modern phone, especially with the charging-limit setting turned on.
- **Charging many times a day** is fine. Small top-ups are kind to a battery.
- **Using the phone while it charges** is fine for light use. Heavy gaming while fast charging is the one to avoid.
