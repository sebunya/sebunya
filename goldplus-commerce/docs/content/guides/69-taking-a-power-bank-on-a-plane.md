A power bank must travel in your cabin baggage, never in a checked suitcase. Airlines judge it by watt-hours (Wh), not mAh. Up to 100Wh is generally allowed without approval; from 100Wh to 160Wh you need your airline's approval; above 160Wh is generally not allowed on passenger flights. Rules differ between airlines, so check yours before you go to Entebbe.

# Why cabin baggage only

Power banks contain lithium cells. If a damaged cell overheats in the cabin, the crew can see it and deal with it. In the hold, nobody can. That is why spare lithium batteries, including power banks, are not allowed in checked luggage.

If you check in a bag with a power bank inside, expect it to be found at screening and removed. Pack it in your hand luggage from the start.

# Find the watt-hours on the label

Look on the body of the power bank for a figure in **Wh**. If it is printed, that is the number the airline cares about.

If only mAh and a voltage are printed, you can work it out: **mAh × volts ÷ 1,000 = Wh.**

As worked examples only:

- 10,000mAh at 3.7V = 37Wh.
- 20,000mAh at 3.7V = 74Wh.

Use the voltage printed on your unit, not 3.7V, if it is different. If the label shows no capacity or voltage at all, security staff may not be able to confirm it is allowed, so a clearly labelled power bank is the safer one to fly with.

# The limits in plain terms

- **Up to 100Wh:** generally allowed in cabin baggage without approval.
- **Over 100Wh up to 160Wh:** needs approval from your airline before you fly. Ask when you book, not at the gate.
- **Over 160Wh:** generally not allowed on passenger aircraft.

Airlines can also limit how many power banks you carry, and some now restrict using or charging a power bank during the flight. These rules change, so check your airline's website for the current position on the day you travel.

# Packing tips for the airport

1. **Charge it before you leave home,** so it is ready for the journey and any delays.
2. **Protect the ports.** Keep it in a pouch or pocket where keys and coins cannot touch the connectors.
3. **Have it easy to reach.** Security may ask to see it.
4. **Do not fly with a damaged one.** A swollen, cracked or very hot power bank should not travel at all.
5. **Keep it with you on board.** Do not put it in a bag that may be moved to the hold at the gate. If a cabin bag is taken at the door, remove the power bank first.

# Printed symbols are not approvals

A plane icon or a phrase like "flight safe" printed on a box is marketing, not airline approval. Only the Wh figure on the unit and your airline's rules decide what you can carry.

# Choose a power bank for travel

A compact 10,000mAh unit is easy to carry and pack. A 20,000mAh unit gives more charge for long journeys and layovers. Check the Wh figure on the exact unit either way.
