The safest way to listen is simple: keep the volume moderate, take regular breaks, and do not turn the sound up to beat background noise. Look after your earbuds the same simple way: keep them clean, dry and in their case. Here is how.

# Safe listening: the basics

Health organisations, including the World Health Organization, give the same everyday advice for personal audio: keep the volume down, limit how long you listen at high volume, and give your ears rest.

In practice:

- **Keep it moderate.** If someone next to you can hear your music, or you cannot hear a person talking to you at arm's length, it is probably too loud.
- **Take breaks.** Take your earbuds out now and then during long listening sessions, especially on long journeys.
- **Do not fight the noise.** On a loud taxi or near a generator it is tempting to turn the volume right up. Instead, wait for a quieter moment or get a better fit.
- **Watch the warning signs.** Ringing in the ears, or sounds seeming muffled after listening, are signs it was too loud or too long. Give your ears a rest, and if it does not clear, see a health worker.

# Let your phone help

Most phones have tools that make safe listening easier.

- **Android:** many phones show a warning when you raise headphone volume past a certain level, and some let you set a maximum volume. Search your Settings for **volume limit** or **hearing**.
- **iPhone:** look under Settings, then Sounds & Haptics, then **Headphone Safety**, for options to reduce loud audio.

Menu names vary, so use the search bar in Settings if you cannot find them.

# Stay aware when you are out

Being able to hear traffic is a safety matter too. When walking near roads or riding as a boda passenger, keep the volume low enough to hear horns and engines, or use one earbud only. # Keeping your earbuds clean

Earbuds sit in your ears every day, so earwax, sweat and dust build up. Clean them regularly.

**What you need:** a soft, dry, lint-free cloth, a dry cotton bud and a soft, clean brush such as a new small paintbrush or soft toothbrush.

1. **Wipe the outside** of each earbud with the dry cloth.
2. **Brush the speaker mesh** gently to loosen earwax. Hold the earbud with the mesh facing down so debris falls out, not in.
3. **Clean the charging contacts** on the earbuds and inside the case with a dry cotton bud. Dirty contacts are a common reason an earbud stops charging.
4. **Wipe out the case**, including the lid and the slots.

If your earbuds have removable silicone tips, take them off and clean them separately, then let them dry fully before putting them back.

# What to avoid

- **Water.** Do not rinse earbuds or the case, or soak them, unless the manual says they are made for it.
- **Sharp objects.** Pins and needles can puncture the speaker mesh.
- **Harsh chemicals.** Strong cleaners can damage the finish and seals.
- **Pushing debris in.** Gentle brushing is better than poking.
- **Sharing too often.** Earbuds sit in the ear, so it is more hygienic not to share them, or to clean them before and after.

# Daily habits

- Put earbuds back in their case when you take them out, rather than into a pocket.
- Wipe them off after exercise or a hot, sweaty day.
- Keep wired earphones like the [GP-H06](/products/goldplus-earphones-gp-h06-type-pin) loosely coiled, and wipe the earpieces the same way.
