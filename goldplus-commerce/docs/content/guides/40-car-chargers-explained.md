A car charger plugs into the round power socket in your car, often called the cigarette lighter socket or the 12V socket. It takes the car's power and turns it into USB power that your phone can use, the same way a wall charger turns mains power into USB power.

# The 12V socket

Most cars have at least one round socket on the dashboard or between the front seats. It is fed by the car's electrical system.

- **Cars and most small vehicles** use a 12V system. With the engine running, the voltage is usually a little higher than 12V because the alternator is charging the battery.
- **Many trucks, buses and some larger vehicles** use 24V.
- **Some sockets are live all the time,** while others only work when the ignition is on. If your charger's light goes off when you switch off the engine, your socket is the second kind.

Check that a car charger is made for your vehicle's voltage. Many are made to work on both 12V and 24V.

# What the car charger does

Inside a car charger is a small converter. It takes the car's 12V or 24V and steps it down to what the phone needs:

- **5V** for standard USB charging.
- **Higher voltages** such as 9V or 12V for fast charging, if the charger supports a standard like USB PD or Quick Charge and the phone asks for it.

The phone still controls how much it takes. A fast car charger is safe for a phone that only supports normal charging.

# Ports and fast charging

Car chargers usually have one or two USB ports:

- **USB-A:** often supports Quick Charge (QC) on fast models.
- **USB-C:** often supports USB Power Delivery (PD). An iPhone fast charges from a PD USB-C port with a cable that has USB-C at the charger end.

On a two-port car charger, the power is shared when both are in use. Read the label for each port.

# Reading a car charger's label

A car charger's label is like a wall charger's:

- **Input:** for example DC 12–24V. This tells you which vehicles it suits.
- **Output for each port:** in volts and amps. Multiply them for watts.

# Engine on, engine off

- **Plug in after starting the engine.** Starting the engine can make the voltage dip and jump for a moment.
- **Do not drain the car battery.** If your socket stays live with the engine off, a phone charging for hours can slowly drain the car battery. Unplug when you park for a long time.
- **Remove the charger when you leave the car,** so it is not in the hot sun on a parked car's dashboard.

# The cable

The cable matters in a car as much as at home. Cables in cars get bent, trapped in doors and baked in the sun. Use a cable that matches the port and keep a spare in the glovebox. A braided cable such as our [GP-L14T USB-C cable](/products/goldplus-cable-gp-l14t) suits a USB-A port and a Type-C phone.

# Signs of a problem

- The charger's light flickers or goes off on bumps: the socket may be loose or dirty.
- The charger gets very hot, smells or the plastic softens: unplug it and stop using it.
- The car's fuse for the socket blows: have the socket checked before using it again.
