A fake charger is often easy to miss on the shelf, but it usually gives itself away when you look closely. Check the weight, the label, the plug and the packaging, and verify the product's code before you rely on it. If anything looks wrong, do not use it.

A charger sits between mains electricity and a phone you carry against your body. It is worth two minutes of checking.

# Why fake chargers are a risk

A genuine charger has insulation and protection circuits that keep mains voltage away from the USB side, and limit current and heat if something goes wrong. A fake may skip or cut corners on these parts to save cost. The risks include:

- Overheating and melting of the case or plug.
- An unstable output that can upset a phone's charging circuit.
- Poor insulation between the mains side and the USB side.

You cannot see inside, so you judge from the outside.

# Signs to check

## Weight and feel

- **Too light.** A charger with almost no weight for its stated power may be missing parts.
- **Rattles.** Loose parts inside are a bad sign.
- **Seams and finish.** Gaps along the case join, rough edges or a case that flexes when squeezed suggest poor manufacture.

## The label

- **Missing or vague output lines.** A genuine charger prints its input and output clearly, for example 100-240V ~50/60Hz on input and each output profile in volts and amps.
- **Misspellings and smudged print.** Spelling errors, blurry text or labels that rub off easily are common on fakes.
- **Numbers that do not add up.** Volts times amps should roughly match the watts claimed. A huge watt figure on a tiny, light charger is suspicious.
- **Box and label disagree.** Marketing on the box is not the specification. The label on the charger is what to trust.

## The plug and pins

- **Pins that wobble or look thin.** Pins should be solid and firmly fixed.
- **Wrong spacing.** A UK-style plug that is hard to insert, or loose in a good socket, is a warning sign.

## Packaging and price

- **Poor packaging,** missing model numbers or no maker's details.
- **A price far below what similar genuine products cost.** A big saving often means missing parts.

## In use

- **Hot to the touch** after a short charge, a burning smell, buzzing, or the phone repeatedly connecting and disconnecting. Unplug it and stop using it.

# Safer buying habits

- Buy from a shop with a fixed address you can return to.
- Read the label in the shop before you pay.
- Keep your receipt.
- Verify the code before you rely on the product.
- If a charger has ever overheated or sparked, throw it away safely rather than keep it as a spare.
