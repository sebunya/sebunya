Most slow charging has a simple cause you can check at home. Start with the cable, because it is the most common culprit, then work down this list. You usually do not need a new phone, and often not even a new charger.

# 1. A worn or damaged cable

Cables take a beating: bent at the plug, pulled from the socket, squeezed in bags. Inside, the thin wires break bit by bit, and the cable carries less current or drops out of fast-charge mode.

**Check:** look for a loose plug, a split sleeve or a kink near either end. Try a different cable you know works. If charging speeds up, replace the old one. A braided cable such as our [GP-L14T USB-C cable](/products/goldplus-cable-gp-l14t) is built for more bending.

# 2. A dirty or loose charging port

Pocket fluff and dust collect in the phone's port. They stop the plug from seating fully, so the connection is poor.

**Check:** shine a torch into the port. If you see lint, switch the phone off and gently lift it out with a wooden toothpick. Never use metal pins or blow hard into it. If the plug still wobbles after cleaning, the port may need a repair.

# 3. The charger and phone do not share a fast-charging standard

Fast charging only works when both sides speak the same language, such as USB PD or Quick Charge. If they do not, the phone falls back to normal speed. It is safe, just slow.

**Check:** look up your phone's spec for its standard, then read the charger's label for that standard on the port you use. An iPhone, for example, needs a USB-C PD port and a cable with USB-C at the charger end to fast charge.

# 4. Using the wrong port on a multi-port charger

On a dual-port charger, the USB-A and USB-C ports often support different standards and different power. Two phones connected at once also share the power.

**Check:** read the label for each port and for both ports together. Try charging one phone alone on the port that suits it best.

# 5. A charger with too little power

An old 5V⎓1A charger gives only 5W. A phone with a large battery can take many hours on it.

**Check:** read the output line on the label. Volts times amps gives the watts of each listed profile. If it is much lower than your phone's spec, a stronger charger will help. A charger with more power than the phone needs is safe; the phone takes only what it can handle.

# 6. Heat

Phones protect their batteries by slowing charging when they get hot. Charging in direct sun, on a car dashboard, under a pillow or while gaming all raise the temperature.

**Check:** charge on a hard, cool surface in the shade. Take off a thick case if the phone feels hot. You will often see the speed pick up once it cools.

# 7. The phone is busy, or nearly full

Streaming video, using hotspot, or playing games while charging uses power as fast as it comes in. Also, every phone charges fast up to around the middle and then slows down deliberately near full to protect the battery.

**Check:** turn on airplane mode or leave the phone alone for a while. Judge speed in the first half of the charge, not the last part.

# When it is the battery

If none of the above helps, the battery itself may be worn. Signs include the phone dying early in the day, switching off at 20% or 30%, or getting unusually hot. A swollen battery, where the back bulges or the screen lifts, is a fire risk: stop using and charging the phone and have it looked at. Use the [battery finder](/battery-finder) to find the battery for your exact model.

# Quick fix list

- Try a different, good cable.
- Clean the port with the phone switched off.
- Use the port that matches your phone's standard.
- Charge one phone at a time on a shared charger.
- Keep it cool and leave it alone while it charges.
