A wireless power bank charges your phone when you lay the phone on it, with no cable. A magnetic power bank goes one step further: magnets hold it to the back of the phone, so you can keep using the phone while it charges. Both only work if your phone supports wireless charging, and a magnetic one also needs a phone or case that supports magnetic attachment.

# How wireless charging works

Inside the power bank is a flat coil. Inside a wireless-capable phone is another coil. When the two line up, the power bank's coil creates a magnetic field that the phone's coil turns back into electricity to charge the battery.

The common standard for this is **Qi**. Many recent phones support it, but plenty of phones, especially budget models, do not. Check your phone's specifications, or look in Settings for anything about wireless charging, before you buy.

# What "magnetic" adds

With an ordinary wireless pad, the phone has to sit still in the right spot. If it slides off the coil, charging stops. A magnetic power bank uses a ring of magnets to snap into the right position on the back of the phone and stay there.

Two separate questions decide whether it will work for you:

- **Does your phone support wireless charging?**
- **Does your phone, or its case, support magnetic attachment?** Many iPhones from the iPhone 12 onwards have magnets built in. On other phones, a magnetic case or a magnetic ring stuck to the back may be needed.

# Wireless is convenient, a cable is faster

Wireless charging loses more energy as heat than a cable does. That has three practical effects:

- **It is usually slower** than a good wired connection.
- **The phone and power bank get warmer.** Remove thick cases if charging seems slow or warm, and keep both out of the sun.
- **You get less charge into the phone** from the same power bank than you would by cable.

So the sensible approach is to use wireless when convenience matters, such as walking, on a call or on a crowded taxi, and a cable when you want the most charge or the fastest top-up.

# Cases, cards and coins

Wireless charging works through most thin phone cases. Thick cases, metal plates, ring holders and cards tucked in the back of the case can stop it working or make it slow. Never leave bank cards or ID cards with magnetic strips between a magnetic power bank and your phone.
