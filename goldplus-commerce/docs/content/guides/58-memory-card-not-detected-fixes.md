When a memory card is not detected, start with the simple things: switch off, take the card out, check it sits correctly, and try again. Then test the card in another device to learn whether the problem is the card or the phone. Most of the time it is one of a handful of causes, listed below in the order to check them.

> If the card holds photos you cannot replace, do not format it while troubleshooting. Formatting erases everything. Try every other step first.

# 1. Restart and reseat

1. Switch the phone off.
2. Take out the SIM tray and the card.
3. Check the card sits flat in its space, the right way up, with the metal contacts facing the way the tray shows.
4. Put it back, close the tray fully, and switch on.

A card that has shifted slightly in the tray, often after a drop or a bumpy ride, is a very common cause.

# 2. Clean the contacts

Dust and pocket lint get into card slots. Gently wipe the card's gold contacts with a clean, dry, soft cloth. Do not use water. Blow lightly into the tray space to clear dust. Do not push anything metal into the slot.

# 3. Try it in another device

Put the card in another phone, or in a card reader on a computer.

- **Works elsewhere:** the card is fine. The problem is your phone's slot, its settings, or a format it does not read.
- **Fails everywhere:** the card itself may be damaged or failing.

# 4. Check the size your device supports

Older phones, radios, dashcams and cameras often have a maximum card size. A device that reads microSDHC (up to 32GB) may not recognise a 64GB microSDXC card at all. Check the manual or the maker's website for the largest card your device accepts.

# 5. Check the format

A card formatted on one device may use a format another device cannot read. Cards larger than 32GB usually come formatted as exFAT, which some older devices do not support.

If the card has nothing you need on it, format it **in the device you will use it in**, from that device's own settings (on Android: Settings, then Storage, then the SD card). This sets it up in the format that device expects.

# 6. Check Android settings

- In **Settings, then Storage**, see whether the card is listed but **unmounted** or **ejected**. If so, tap Mount.
- If the phone says the card is **corrupted** or asks to set it up, copy anything you can with a computer first, before letting the phone format it.
- If the card was once set up as **internal storage** in another phone, it is locked to that phone and must be reformatted to use elsewhere.

# 7. On a computer

- Try a different USB port or a different card reader.
- In Windows, open Disk Management to see whether the card appears without a drive letter. If it does, it can be given one there.
- If the card is found but asks to be formatted, recover anything important first, if you can.

# Signs it is time to replace the card

- It fails in every device and every reader.
- It keeps disconnecting or corrupting files after a fresh format.
- Files open as grey or broken images, or vanish after being saved. This can also be a sign of a fake card that claims more space than it has.
