To find the right battery for an Infinix or itel phone, start with two things: the phone's exact model number from Settings, and the code printed on the old battery. Match those. The name on the box and the size of the battery are not enough, because similar phones can use different batteries and similar batteries can have different codes.

# Step 1: get the exact model number

The model number is more precise than the marketing name.

1. Open **Settings**, then **About phone**.
2. Note the **model** shown there. Infinix model numbers are often a short code such as X followed by digits; itel phones show their own model code.
3. Write it down exactly, including any letter at the end.

If the phone will not switch on, check the box, your receipt, or a sticker under the battery cover.

# Step 2: find the battery code if you can

Many itel phones, and some Infinix phones, have a removable back. On those:

1. Switch the phone off.
2. Take off the back cover.
3. Read the code on the battery label. These often start with **BL-**, for example BL-49FT.

Copy the whole code. One letter can make it a different battery.

On sealed phones, do not prise the back open. The model number from Step 1 is enough to search.

# Step 3: search, do not guess

Use the [battery finder](/battery-finder) and search by your model number or the battery code. Our finder lists fits from our own checks and our supplier's compatibility list. Each fit says where it comes from: "Verified fit" means we checked it ourselves; "Fits, from our supplier's compatibility list" means it comes from that list.

What to avoid:

- **Guessing from the phone's name.** A name can cover several versions sold at different times.
- **Matching by shape.** A battery that physically fits can have a different connector, voltage or contact layout.
- **Matching by mAh.** Capacity tells you how much a battery stores, not which phone it fits.

# Step 4: compare before fitting

When the new battery arrives:

- Compare its code with the code on your old battery. They should match.
- Check the nominal voltage on both labels. It should be the same.
- Do not fit a battery if anything looks wrong, such as a bent connector or a puffed-up pack.

# If your phone is itel and very basic

Many itel feature phones and basic models use small removable batteries. The same rule applies: the code on the battery is what to match. These batteries are often easy to swap yourself. Switch off, change the battery, and refit the cover.

# Signs you need a new battery

- The phone no longer lasts your normal day.
- It switches off at 20% or 30%.
- It gets hot while idle.
- The back is bulging or the battery looks puffy. That is a swollen battery: stop using and charging it, and keep it away from heat.

# Care for the new battery

- Charge with a charger that has its output printed on it, such as 5V⎓2A.
- Keep the phone out of direct sun and hot cars.
- Avoid running it flat often.
- For the first few days, the percentage may be a little off while the phone learns the new battery. That settles with normal use.
