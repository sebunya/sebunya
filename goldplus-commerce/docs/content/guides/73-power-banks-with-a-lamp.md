A power bank with a built-in lamp gives you two things from one device: light when the power goes, and charge for your phone. It means you no longer drain your phone using its torch, and you have one thing to grab when the lights go off. The only catch is that the lamp and the phone share the same battery, so you balance the two.

# Why not just use the phone torch?

The phone torch works, but it uses the battery you need for calls, messages and mobile money. In a long outage, a phone used as a torch all evening may be flat when you most need it. A separate lamp keeps the phone free, and keeps its battery for communication.

A lamp built into the power bank also means one less item to find in the dark, and one less device to keep charged.

# Handy uses at home

- **During power cuts:** light a room for cooking, eating or getting children ready for bed.
- **Evening homework:** a steady light on the table while the power is off.
- **Finding your way:** to the gate, the toilet or the meter at night.
- **Checking the fuse box or meter** when the power drops, without holding a phone in your mouth.

# Handy uses away from home

- **Travel upcountry,** where you may arrive after dark or stay where power is unreliable.
- **Camping and outdoor events,** where the lamp lights the tent and the power bank charges the phones.
- **Market stalls and night shifts,** where a little light and a phone top-up both help.

# Balance light and charge

Light and phone charging both draw on the same cells. A few simple habits make the battery go further:

1. **Switch the lamp off when you leave the room.** It is easy to forget it is on.
2. **Watch the charge display.** If it drops faster than you expect, reduce lamp use and keep some charge for the phone.
3. **Decide what matters most tonight.** If the phone has to last until morning, give it priority.
4. **Recharge as soon as the power returns,** so the lamp is ready for the next outage.

# Safety notes

- Do not stare into the lamp or shine it in someone's eyes at close range.
- Keep the power bank on a hard, stable surface when it is lighting a room, not on a bed or under cloth.
- Keep it away from candles, paraffin lamps and stoves.
- If it gets unusually hot, swells or smells of burning, stop using it.
