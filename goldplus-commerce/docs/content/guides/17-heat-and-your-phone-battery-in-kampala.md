Heat ages a phone battery faster than anything else you do to it. A phone that regularly gets hot, in the sun, in a parked car or while charging under a pillow, loses battery capacity sooner than one kept cool. You do not need to baby your phone, but avoiding the hottest moments of the day makes a real difference over a year or two.

# Why heat matters

Inside a lithium-ion battery, the chemical reactions that store energy also slowly wear the battery out. Heat speeds those reactions up. A battery that spends a lot of time hot loses capacity faster and is more likely to swell.

Phone makers publish the temperature range their phones are designed for. Apple, for example, says iPhones work best at ambient temperatures between 0°C and 35°C. Outside that, phones may dim the screen, stop charging or show a temperature warning to protect themselves.

# Where phones get hottest in daily life

- **On a car dashboard.** Glass and dark plastic in the sun get much hotter than the air outside. A phone used for directions on the dashboard, while also charging, is in one of the worst spots there is.
- **In a parked car.** Even with the windows cracked, a car in the sun heats up fast. Do not leave your phone inside.
- **In direct sun.** On a café table outdoors, on a market stall, or in a shirt pocket on a long walk in the afternoon.
- **In a back pocket on a boda.** Sun on one side, body heat on the other, and sometimes a hot seat underneath.
- **Under a pillow or on a mattress while charging.** The heat from charging has nowhere to go.
- **Gaming or video calls while fast charging.** Two sources of heat at once.

# How to tell when it is too hot

Warm is normal, especially while charging, streaming or using mobile data on a weak signal. Too hot looks like this:

- The phone is uncomfortable to hold.
- It shows a temperature warning or stops charging.
- The screen dims by itself and will not brighten.
- It gets hot while sitting idle.

If that happens, stop charging, close heavy apps, take off the case, and move the phone into shade. Do not put it in a fridge or freezer; sudden cooling can cause condensation inside.

# Simple ways to keep it cooler

- **Mount it away from the windscreen** in the car, or cover it from direct sun. Charge with a proper car charger, such as the [GoldPlus GP-CA02](/products/goldplus-car-charger-gp-ca02), which lists its output for each port, rather than an unmarked one.
- **Take it with you** when you leave the car, even for ten minutes.
- **Keep it in a bag** instead of a pocket on hot afternoons outdoors.
- **Charge on a hard surface** indoors, away from windows and sun.
- **Take off a thick case** while charging if the phone gets warm.
- **Pause the game** while the phone fast charges, or charge first and play after.
- **Lower screen brightness** in the sun only as much as you need; full brightness generates heat.

# Power cuts and hot rooms

During a power cut in the afternoon, a closed room can get hot, and so does anything charging in it. If you rely on a power bank, keep the power bank cool too. The same lithium-ion rules apply to it. A model with a charge display, such as the [GoldPlus GP-P07](/products/goldplus-power-bank-gp-p07-pb), lets you see its level without guessing.

# When heat has done damage

If the back of the phone has started to lift, the screen is pushing out, or the battery looks puffy, the battery has swollen. Stop using and charging it, keep it away from heat, and have it replaced.
