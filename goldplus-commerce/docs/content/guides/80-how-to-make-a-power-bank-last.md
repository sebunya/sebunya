Every power bank slowly loses capacity as its lithium cells age, but how you treat it makes a big difference to how fast. The three habits that matter most are keeping it cool, not running it completely flat all the time, and storing it about half charged when you will not use it for a while.

# Keep it cool

Heat ages lithium-ion cells faster than anything else. A power bank that lives in hot places loses capacity sooner.

- Keep it out of direct sun, including on dashboards, boda seats and window sills.
- Never leave it in a parked car during the day.
- Charge it on a hard, open surface, not on a bed or under cushions.
- Let it cool down after heavy use before you recharge it.

# Avoid the extremes of charge

Lithium-ion cells are under the most strain when they are completely empty or sitting at completely full for long periods.

- **Recharge before it is empty.** You do not need to run it down to zero before charging. There is no "memory effect" in lithium-ion cells to train away.
- **Do not leave it on charge for days.** Unplug once it is full.
- **For daily use, a full charge is fine.** It is long periods at 100%, especially when warm, that wear it more.

# Store it the right way

If you will not use a power bank for weeks, such as during a long holiday:

1. Charge or discharge it to about half.
2. Store it in a cool, dry place.
3. Top it up to about half every few months.

A power bank left completely empty for months may refuse to charge again.

# Use good cables and chargers

- A worn, thin or damaged cable wastes energy as heat and strains the ports.
- Use a charger in good condition with a clear output label.
- Pull cables out by the plug, never by the lead.

# Look after the ports and built-in cables

- Keep metal objects such as keys and coins away from the ports.
- Blow or brush dust out of ports gently. Do not push pins or needles inside.
- Do not bend built-in cables sharply at the joint, and tuck them back in their slots after use. Our [GP-P06](/products/goldplus-power-bank-gp-p06-pb), for example, has USB-C and Lightning cables built in.

# Treat it gently

- Do not drop it or carry it loose with heavy objects.
- Keep it dry. A wet power bank should not be charged until it has dried fully.
- Do not sit on it in a back pocket.

# Signs it is time to replace it

- It gives clearly less charge than it used to.
- It empties fast even when not in use.
- It gets hot during normal charging.
- The case bulges, cracks or no longer closes properly. A swollen power bank is a fire risk: stop using and charging it.

When the time comes, do not put an old power bank in household rubbish or a fire. Ask about safe disposal for batteries where you live.
