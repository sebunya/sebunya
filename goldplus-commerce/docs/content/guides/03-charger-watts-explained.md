To understand a charger, read the output profiles printed for the port you will use. Multiplying volts by amps gives the power a listed profile represents. It does not prove the charger delivers that power in every situation, or that several ports can give their individual maximums at the same time.

# Read the output line

Every charger has a printed label with an **Input** line (what it takes from the wall) and an **Output** line (what it gives your phone). The output is written as volts and amps, for example 5V⎓2A.

The sum is: **volts × amps = watts.**

- 5V⎓2A = 10W.
- 9V⎓2.22A = about 20W.
- 12V⎓1.5A = 18W.
- 20V⎓3.25A = 65W, the kind of power a laptop needs.

Many fast chargers list several lines, one for each voltage. **The highest line is the largest profile the charger lists for that port**, and it only applies when the phone and the cable support that mode. These are calculations of listed profiles, not test results for the unit in your hand.

# A port limit is not a shared limit

A charger with two or three ports usually prints a separate line for what the ports can do together. When two devices are connected, the power is shared and each port may drop to a lower profile. Read the per-port lines and the combined line separately, and do not add the port figures together.

# When the box and the label disagree

A large number on the front of a box is marketing. The output table on the charger body, or on the label, is the figure to compare. If the two disagree, ask us to read the label on the exact unit before you order.

# What PD and QC mean

Fast charging only happens when the charger and the phone speak the same language.

- **USB Power Delivery (PD)** works over USB-C. iPhones from the iPhone 8 onwards fast charge with a USB-C PD charger of about 20W. Many Android phones use PD too, including Samsung's Super Fast Charging, which uses a version of PD called PPS.
- **Quick Charge (QC)** is common on Android phones and often uses a USB-A port.

If the charger and the phone do not share a standard, the phone still charges safely, just at normal speed.

# Is a bigger charger safe for my phone?

Yes. A phone only draws the power it can handle. A 65W charger will charge a phone that supports 20W at up to 20W. The extra headroom is useful if you also charge a tablet or a laptop.

# The cable matters too

- For fast charging over USB-C, use a USB-C cable in good condition.
- Most USB-C cables carry up to 60W. For 100W you need a cable rated for 100W (5A).
- A frayed or loose cable slows charging and can get hot. Replace it.
