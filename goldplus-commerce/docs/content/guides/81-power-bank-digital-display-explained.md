A digital display on a power bank tells you how much charge it has left, usually as a percentage. It replaces the guesswork of four small lights with a number, so you know whether you can charge your phone fully, or whether the power bank needs recharging first. The number is an estimate, not an exact measurement, and that explains most of its odd moments.

# What the number means

Most displays show the power bank's own remaining charge, from 0 to 100. It is not the amount your phone will receive. Energy is lost as heat when the power bank charges your phone, so 50% on the display does not mean half a phone charge or half the label's mAh.

Some displays also show an icon or animation while the power bank is charging or discharging. What each symbol means varies by model, so check the manual for yours.

# How it is worked out

The power bank cannot see inside its cells. It estimates the charge level from measurements such as the cell voltage and the current going in and out. That is why the number behaves the way it does:

- **It can drop faster at first** when a phone starts drawing a lot of power, then settle.
- **It can stall at 99%** while the last part of the charge goes in slowly.
- **It can jump** after the power bank rests, as the cell voltage recovers.
- **It can drift as the power bank ages,** because older cells hold less than when they were new.

Small jumps are normal. A number that drops from high to zero suddenly, or a display that flickers constantly, is not. Stop using a power bank that behaves like that and gets hot.

# Using the display to plan

**Before you leave home.** If the display shows a low number, recharge before you go. A glance in the morning saves a dead phone in the evening.

**During a power cut.** Use the number to share fairly. If the household has 60% left and an evening ahead, top phones up to a useful level instead of filling one completely. If your power bank has a lamp, watch how fast the lamp brings the number down.

**While recharging.** The display tells you when the power bank is full, so you can unplug it rather than leaving it on charge for days.

**Over months.** If the same full charge seems to give your phone less than it used to, the cells are ageing. That is a sign to plan a replacement.

# Quick checks

1. Read the display each morning and after each outage.
2. Recharge before it reaches single digits, not after.
3. Unplug once it shows full.
4. If the reading starts behaving strangely and the power bank gets hot or swells, stop using it.
