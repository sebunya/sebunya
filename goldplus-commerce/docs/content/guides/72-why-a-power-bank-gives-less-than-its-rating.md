A power bank never delivers its full mAh rating to your phone, and that is normal, not a fault. The rating describes the cells inside at their own voltage. Getting that energy into your phone means converting it to USB voltage and then back again inside the phone, and every step loses some energy as heat.

# The rating is stated at cell voltage

The mAh on the box is usually stated at the voltage of the lithium cells inside, which is lower than the voltage USB uses. When the power bank raises that voltage to 5V or more for its output, the mAh number changes, even before any loss.

That is why mAh alone is a poor way to compare. Watt-hours (Wh) describe energy whatever the voltage. As a worked example only: a unit rated 10,000mAh at 3.7V stores 37Wh. The same 37Wh at 5V would be only about 7,400mAh. No energy has gone missing; it is the same energy measured at a different voltage.

# Where the energy is actually lost

On top of the voltage difference, real energy is lost along the way:

- **In the power bank's converter,** which turns cell voltage into USB voltage and warms up as it does.
- **In the cable,** especially a long, thin or damaged one.
- **In the phone's own charging circuit,** which converts USB voltage back to battery voltage.
- **As heat in both batteries,** which is why both get warm.

How much is lost depends on the model, the output mode and the cable. There is no single percentage that applies to every power bank, so be wary of anyone who quotes one as a rule.

# Things that waste even more

- **Wireless charging** loses more energy than a cable, because the coils are not perfectly matched.
- **Using the phone while it charges,** especially for video or games, means some of the energy runs the phone instead of filling it.
- **Heat.** A hot phone or power bank charges less efficiently, and may slow charging to protect itself.
- **Topping up a nearly full phone** uses energy slowly, because phones charge slowly near 100%.
- **Age.** As lithium cells age, they hold less than when they were new.

# Protection keeps some charge back

Power banks are designed not to drain their cells completely flat, because that would damage them. When the display reads zero, there is usually a small reserve the power bank will not hand over. This protects the cells and is part of normal design.

# How to get more out of every charge

1. **Use a cable** when you want the most charge, and wireless when you want convenience.
2. **Use a short, good-quality cable** in good condition.
3. **Let the phone rest** while it charges, or at least avoid heavy apps.
4. **Charge in the shade,** not in the sun or a hot car.
5. **Top up when the phone is low,** rather than constantly from 90%.
