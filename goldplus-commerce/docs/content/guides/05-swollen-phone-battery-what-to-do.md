If your phone battery is swollen, stop using it and stop charging it now. A swollen lithium-ion battery can catch fire, especially if it is pressed, punctured or heated. Switch the phone off if you can do it without pressing hard on the screen, unplug it, and put it somewhere it cannot hurt anyone while you arrange a replacement.

# How to recognise a swollen battery

Swelling happens slowly, so it is easy to miss until it is obvious.

- The back cover bulges or has popped off at one corner.
- The screen lifts away from the frame, often along one edge, or has a new crack you cannot explain.
- The phone rocks when you lay it flat on a table.
- On a phone with a removable back, the battery itself looks puffy, like a small pillow, instead of flat.
- The phone feels thicker or the case no longer fits.

# Why batteries swell

Inside a lithium-ion battery, chemical reactions can produce gas. That gas has nowhere to go, so the soft pouch puffs up. It is more likely in an old battery, one that has been kept hot (a car dashboard, a sunny window, a thick case while charging), one that was damaged by a drop, or one that has been charged with a faulty charger or cable.

Swelling does not mean the battery will catch fire, but it does mean the battery is damaged and the risk is real. It will not go back down by itself.

# What to do in the first hour

1. **Unplug it.** Do not charge a swollen battery, even "just to back up my photos".
2. **Switch it off** if you can, without pressing on the bulge.
3. **Move it somewhere safe.** A hard, non-flammable surface such as a tiled floor or a concrete step, away from beds, curtains, paper and children. Not in a pocket or a handbag.
4. **Keep it cool.** Out of the sun and away from cookers and heaters. Do not put it in a fridge or freezer.
5. **Watch for danger signs.** Hissing, smoke, a sweet chemical smell or rapid heating mean the battery is failing. Keep away from it, keep people away, and if it catches fire, call for help.

# What never to do

- **Do not press, squeeze or try to flatten it.** Pressure can short the battery inside.
- **Do not puncture it** to "let the air out". This can cause a fire within seconds.
- **Do not prise open a sealed phone yourself.** Many phones are glued shut, and a tool slipping into a swollen battery is a real hazard.
- **Do not keep using it** with tape holding the back on.
- **Do not throw it in a bin or a fire.** See the section on getting rid of it below.

# Getting your data and your phone back

If your photos and contacts are backed up to your Google or Apple account, you lose nothing by stopping now. If they are not, do not charge the phone to rescue them. Ask a technician whether the battery can be safely removed or replaced first.

Once a trained person has taken out the swollen battery, the phone itself is often fine. A swollen battery can damage the screen or the frame, so check those once the new battery is in.

# Replacing it

- **Find your exact model number.** If the phone will not switch on, it is often on the box, your receipt, or a sticker under the battery cover.

# Getting rid of the old battery

Do not put a swollen battery in household rubbish or burn it. Cover the metal contacts with tape, put it in a small box or bag on its own, and take it to a phone repair shop or an electronics recycler that accepts batteries. Ask them before you go.
