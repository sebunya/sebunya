iPhone Battery Health tells you two things: how much charge your battery can hold compared with when it was new (Maximum Capacity), and whether the battery can still supply the bursts of power the phone needs (Peak Performance Capability). A lower Maximum Capacity means shorter days. A warning under Peak Performance Capability means the phone has shut down unexpectedly, or could, and is managing performance to prevent it.

# Where to find it

Open **Settings**, then **Battery**, then **Battery Health** (on some iOS versions it is called **Battery Health & Charging**). The exact wording depends on your iPhone model and iOS version.

# Maximum Capacity

This percentage compares what your battery can hold today with what it held when new. A new iPhone shows 100%, or close to it.

- **It goes down over time.** Every lithium-ion battery loses capacity as it is charged and ages. A slow fall is normal.
- **It is not a fuel gauge.** 85% here does not mean the battery is 85% charged. It means a full charge now holds about 85% of the original.
- **What it feels like:** a phone at 80% Maximum Capacity gives you roughly four-fifths of the battery life it had new, with the same use.

Apple publishes how much capacity each iPhone generation is designed to keep after a set number of charge cycles. You can read the figure for your model on Apple's support site.

# Peak Performance Capability

This line tells you whether the battery can still deliver power quickly enough for demanding moments, such as opening the camera with the flash or launching a heavy app.

You will usually see one of these messages:

- **Your battery is currently supporting normal peak performance.** All good.
- **This iPhone has experienced an unexpected shutdown… Performance management has been applied.** The battery could not keep up and the phone switched off. iOS now limits peak performance a little to stop it happening again. You may notice slower app launches.
- **Your battery's health is significantly degraded.** Apple recommends a battery service. Everything still works, but you will notice shorter battery life and possibly slower performance.

If you see the performance-management message, you can turn it off on that screen. The phone may then shut down unexpectedly again. Replacing the battery is the real fix.

# Cycle count on newer iPhones

On recent iPhones, you can also see the battery's cycle count, along with its manufacture date and first use, under **Settings**, then **General**, then **About**. Older models do not show this in Settings.

A cycle is using 100% of the battery's capacity, not one plug-in. Using half today and half tomorrow is one cycle.

# Why your number dropped quickly

- **Heat.** Charging under a pillow, leaving the phone in a car, or long gaming sessions while charging all speed up wear.
- **Long hours at 100% while warm.** Turn on Optimised Battery Charging, or a charging limit if your model has one, under Settings, then Battery.
- **Recalculation.** After some iOS updates, iPhone recalibrates the figure, and it can move up or down a little.

# When to replace the battery

Consider a replacement when:

- The phone no longer lasts your normal day.
- Battery Health shows a performance-management or service message.
- The phone shuts down with charge left.
- The back or screen starts to lift. That is a swollen battery: stop using and charging the phone.

# Find your iPhone battery

iPhone batteries are made for one model, so the exact model matters: an iPhone 13 battery is not the same as an iPhone 13 Pro battery. Find your model under Settings, then General, then About.
