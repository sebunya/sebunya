A power bank's recharge time depends on how much energy it holds and how fast energy goes in. A bigger power bank on a slow charger can take most of a day; a smaller one on a fast input takes much less. To estimate yours, you need two figures: the energy in watt-hours (Wh) and the input watts.

# Find the input line

Every power bank label has an **Input** line as well as an Output line. The input says what the power bank accepts when you charge it, written as volts and amps, for example 5V⎓2A. Multiply them for watts: 5V × 2A = 10W.

Some power banks list more than one input, for example a faster one on USB-C. The faster input only applies when the charger and cable support it too.

If the label does not show an input, check the manual or ask us about the exact unit.

# A simple estimate

Divide the energy by the input watts, then add some extra time, because charging is never perfectly efficient and slows at the end.

As worked examples only, using 3.7V as the cell voltage:

- A 10,000mAh unit holds 37Wh. On a 10W input, 37 ÷ 10 is about 3.7 hours, plus extra.
- A 20,000mAh unit holds 74Wh. On a 10W input, that is about 7.4 hours, plus extra. On an 18W input, about 4 hours, plus extra.

These show how the sum works. They are not timings for any particular model.

# Why the last part takes longer

Lithium-ion cells charge fast while they are low and then slow down as they fill, to protect the cells. So the final stretch towards 100% takes longer than the first half. If you are short of time, a partial charge is still useful.

# What slows recharging down

- **A weak charger.** A 5W or 10W charger takes far longer than a fast one, even if the power bank accepts more.
- **The wrong cable.** A thin, long or damaged cable can limit the power that reaches the power bank.
- **Charging other devices at the same time.** Some power banks can do this, some cannot; either way it slows the recharge.
- **Heat.** Many power banks slow charging when they are warm. Charge in a cool, shaded spot.
- **Low voltage at the socket.** During unstable power, charging may stop and restart.

# How to recharge faster, safely

1. Use a charger whose output matches the power bank's highest input, for example a USB-C PD charger if the power bank has a PD input.
2. Use a good cable rated for that power. For USB-C to USB-C, a cable like the [GP-PD04C](/products/goldplus-cable-gp-pd04c), rated 66W, has headroom.
3. Charge on a hard surface, out of the sun.
4. Do not charge phones from it while it is recharging if you want the fastest result.

# Plan around power cuts

If you expect outages, recharge the power bank as soon as the power returns, before topping up phones that still have some charge. A display that shows the charge level, like the one on the GP-P07, tells you when it is full.
