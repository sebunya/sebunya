Pick 10,000mAh if you want something you will carry every day for one phone. Pick 20,000mAh if you need power for long days, power cuts, travel or more than one device, and you are happy to carry a bigger, heavier unit. Speed is a separate question: the output watts decide that, not the size.

# What doubling the capacity really means

A 20,000mAh power bank stores about twice the energy of a 10,000mAh one, when both are stated at the same voltage. Compare watt-hours (Wh) where the label prints them; that is the fairest comparison.

As a worked example only: at 3.7V, 10,000mAh is 37Wh and 20,000mAh is 74Wh. That is how the sum works, not a claim about any particular model.

Neither size promises a fixed number of phone charges. Energy is lost as heat when the power bank converts its cell voltage to USB voltage, and again inside the phone. Your phone always receives less than the label. To estimate for your own phone, compare its battery energy with the power bank's Wh and allow for that loss.

# When 10,000mAh is the right size

- You use one phone and charge it at home most nights.
- You want something that fits a pocket or a small handbag.
- You mainly need a top-up in the afternoon, at campus or on a short trip.

A smaller unit is also quicker to recharge, simply because there is less to fill. If you would leave a big power bank at home because it is heavy, the small one is the better buy.

# When 20,000mAh is the right size

- Power cuts at home last for hours and you want to keep phones going through them.
- You work long days away from a socket, such as on the road or at a market stall.
- You charge two phones, or a phone and earbuds, from the same power bank.
- You travel upcountry and cannot be sure of power where you are going.

The trade-off is size, weight and a longer recharge time.

# Size does not set the speed

A bigger power bank does not charge your phone faster. Speed comes from the output watts and from the phone, power bank and cable all supporting the same standard, such as USB Power Delivery (PD) over USB-C. They differ in capacity, not in their listed output.

# What about flying?

Both sizes are commonly below the 100Wh line that airlines use for power banks in cabin baggage, but do not assume it. Read the Wh figure on the exact unit, keep it in your hand luggage, and check your airline's rules before you fly.

# A quick way to decide

Ask yourself three things:

1. **How long is your longest normal day away from power?** A few hours points to 10,000mAh; a full day or a long outage points to 20,000mAh.
2. **How many devices?** One phone suits the smaller size; two or more suit the bigger one.
3. **Where will it live?** A pocket suits the smaller size; a bag, a desk or a car suits the bigger one.
