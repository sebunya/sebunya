For most people, a phone battery starts to feel noticeably weaker after two or three years of daily use. It does not fail on a set date. It loses capacity a little at a time, so a phone that lasted a full day when new slowly becomes a phone that needs a top-up by evening. How fast that happens depends mostly on heat, how often you charge, and how you use the phone.

There are two ways to think about battery life, and they are easy to mix up:

- **Battery life per charge:** how many hours you get from 100% to empty.
- **Battery lifespan:** how many months or years before the battery holds too little to be useful.

This guide is about the second.

# Why batteries wear out

Phone batteries are lithium-ion. Every time lithium moves in and out of the battery's materials, a tiny amount of the battery's ability to store charge is lost. Over hundreds of charges, that adds up. The battery also ages simply with time, even in a drawer, and much faster when it is warm.

As it wears, two things happen:

- **It holds less.** A battery that started at its full rated capacity may hold a good deal less after a few years.
- **It struggles under load.** An old battery cannot deliver bursts of power as easily, which is why older phones shut down suddenly at 20% or 30%.

# What a charge cycle is

A charge cycle is using 100% of the battery's capacity, not one plug-in. Using 50% today and 50% tomorrow is one cycle, even if you charged twice.

Phone makers design batteries to keep most of their capacity for a certain number of cycles. Apple, for example, publishes figures for its iPhones, and you can read them on Apple's support site. For other brands, check the maker's website or manual.

# What shortens a battery's life

- **Heat.** This is the biggest one. A phone left on a car dashboard, in a sunny window or under a pillow while charging ages faster. Kampala afternoons are warm enough; do not add to it.
- **Sitting at 100% for long periods, especially when warm.** A full battery that is also hot ages faster than one that is cool and part-charged.
- **Running it flat often.** Letting the phone die completely, again and again, stresses the battery.
- **Heavy use while charging.** Gaming or video calls while fast charging makes the phone hot from two directions at once.
- **Poor chargers and cables.** A charger with no clear output label, or a frayed cable, can charge unevenly and run hot.
- **Physical damage.** A drop can damage a battery even if the screen survives.

# What does not matter as much as people think

- **Charging "too often".** Topping up from 40% to 80% is fine. Small top-ups are gentler than full cycles.
- **"Training" a new battery.** Modern lithium batteries do not need to be run flat and charged full several times when new.
- **Using a bigger charger.** A phone only draws the power it can handle. A good charger with a higher rating is not a problem in itself.

# Signs it is time

- The phone no longer lasts your normal day.
- It switches off with charge showing.
- It gets unusually hot.
- The back or screen starts to lift. This means a swollen battery: stop using and charging the phone straight away.

# Getting more years from your battery

- Keep the phone out of direct sun and hot cars.
- Take off a thick case if the phone gets hot while charging.
- Try to keep it roughly between 20% and 80% day to day when that is easy, without worrying about it.
