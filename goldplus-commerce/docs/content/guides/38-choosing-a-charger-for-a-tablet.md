A tablet needs more from a charger than most phones, simply because its battery is bigger. The quickest way to choose is to read the label on the charger that came with the tablet, or its specification page, and pick a charger that gives at least that much on the right port, in the right standard, with a matching cable.

A small phone charger will still charge a tablet safely. It may just take most of the day.

# Why tablets are different

A tablet battery is usually several times the size of a phone battery. At the same charging power, it takes several times as long to fill. A charger that tops up a phone in an hour or two can leave a tablet only partly charged by evening.

That is why many tablets come with a stronger charger than a phone of the same brand, and why an old 5W cube feels painfully slow on one.

# Step 1: check the tablet's port

- **USB-C:** used on most newer tablets, including recent iPads and most current Android tablets.
- **Lightning:** used on some older iPads.
- **Micro-USB:** found on some older or budget Android tablets.

The port decides the cable and whether USB Power Delivery (PD) is possible. PD needs USB-C at the charger end of the cable.

# Step 2: find the power it needs

Look at the original charger's output line. Multiply volts by amps for the highest line to get the watts. Or search your tablet's exact model for its charging specification.

If you no longer have the original charger, the tablet's manual or the maker's website will usually tell you the recommended charger.

# Step 3: check the charger for that port

- For a **USB-C tablet**, look for a USB-C port with **PD**, and output lines at least as high as the original charger's.
- For a **Lightning iPad**, a USB-C PD charger with a USB-C to Lightning cable is the usual fast option.
- For a **Micro-USB tablet**, a USB-A charger with a good USB-A to Micro-USB cable. These tablets usually charge at 5V, so the amps on the 5V line matter most.

A charger with more power than the tablet needs is safe. The tablet takes only what it asks for.

# Step 4: the cable

- Use a cable with the right connectors at both ends.
- Most USB-C to USB-C cables carry up to 60W, which is plenty for most tablets.
- A longer cable helps if you use the tablet on the sofa or in bed while it charges. Our [GP-L12 USB-C to USB-C cable](/products/goldplus-cable-gp-l12) is 2 m and braided.
- Replace a worn or loose cable. Tablets often get used while charging, and the cable takes the strain.

# One charger for a tablet and a phone

A multi-port charger lets you charge both from one socket, which is handy for a student's desk or a family sitting room. Remember that the power is shared when both are connected. If the tablet needs a full charge quickly, give it the charger alone for a while.

# Tablets for children and school

- Use a charger with a firm plug and an undamaged cable.
- Charge on a table, not on a bed or under cushions.
- Do not let children use the tablet in bed while it charges under the covers.
- Unplug during power cuts and wait for the supply to settle before plugging back in.
