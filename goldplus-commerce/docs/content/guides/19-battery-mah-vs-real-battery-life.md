A phone's mAh figure tells you how much charge its battery holds. It does not tell you how long the phone will last on that charge. Two phones with the same 5,000mAh battery can give very different days, because the screen, the chip, the software, the mobile signal and how you use the phone decide how fast that charge is spent.

# What mAh means

mAh stands for milliamp-hours. It is a measure of electric charge: how much current a battery can supply for how long. A bigger number means the battery holds more, at the same voltage.

That last part matters. To compare the actual energy in two batteries, you need watt-hours (Wh):

**mAh × nominal voltage ÷ 1,000 = Wh**

As a worked example only: 5,000mAh at 3.87V is about 19.4Wh. Phone batteries all sit at similar voltages, so for phones, mAh is a fair rough comparison. For power banks it is not, because a power bank's capacity is usually stated at the voltage of its cells, not the voltage it delivers.

# What really decides your battery life

## The screen

The screen is often the biggest user of power. Brightness, size, resolution and refresh rate all matter. A large, bright, fast-refreshing screen spends charge quickly.

## The chip

Newer, more efficient processors do the same work with less power. A phone with a smaller battery and an efficient chip can outlast a phone with a bigger battery and an older chip.

## The signal

A phone on a weak signal raises its radio power to stay connected. If you spend the day where coverage is patchy, such as in a basement office, a village upcountry or on a bus between towns, the battery drains faster than in the middle of Kampala with full bars.

## Mobile data, Wi-Fi and location

Streaming video over mobile data, keeping location on for many apps, and constantly searching for Wi-Fi all add up.

## Apps and software

Some apps run in the background, refresh constantly or keep the phone awake. A software update can also change battery life, for better or worse.

## The battery's age

Every lithium-ion battery loses capacity over time. A two-year-old 5,000mAh battery holds less than it did when new. The mAh on the label is what it held when new, not what it holds today.

## Heat

A hot phone works less efficiently, and heat wears the battery faster. Both shorten your day.

# Why a "bigger" replacement battery is not the answer

A replacement battery should match the original's code and voltage. A higher mAh number printed on a battery made for the same phone should be treated with caution: the battery still has to fit the same space. If you want more hours, a power bank is the honest way to get them.

# Getting more from the mAh you have

- Lower the screen brightness, or use auto-brightness.
- Shorten the screen timeout.
- Turn off location for apps that do not need it.
- Check the battery usage list in Settings and deal with any app using far more than its share.
- Use Wi-Fi where it is reliable instead of mobile data.
- In areas with no signal, turn on airplane mode rather than letting the phone search all day.
