For a long drive to Gulu, Mbarara, Mbale or anywhere upcountry, choose a car charger that fits your vehicle's socket, has enough ports for everyone in the car, and fast charges the phone you use for maps. Then add two things people forget: a spare cable and a charged power bank.

# Why long trips are hard on a phone

On a long journey, a phone works harder than usual:

- **Maps with the screen on** for hours use a lot of power.
- **Weak signal** on some stretches makes the phone search for a network, which drains the battery faster.
- **Heat** in a car in the afternoon sun slows charging and wears the battery.

A charger that only gives a basic 5V can struggle to keep up with a phone running maps with the screen bright. A fast charger helps the battery hold or gain charge while you drive.

# Step 1: match the socket

- Most cars use a **12V** socket.
- Many buses, trucks and some larger vehicles use **24V**.

Check the charger's input line. Our [GP-CA02 car charger](/products/goldplus-car-charger-gp-ca02) lists input DC 12–24V and our [GP-CA03](/products/goldplus-car-charger-gp-ca03) is made for the 12/24V socket, so both suit either kind.

# Step 2: count the ports

Think about who will be charging:

- **Just you:** one good fast port is enough.
- **Driver and a passenger:** two ports, so the navigator's phone and a second phone can charge together.
- **A full car:** a two-port charger plus a power bank passed around the back seat.

On a two-port charger, the power is shared when both are in use. Give the phone running the map the better port.

# Step 3: fast charging for the phone that matters

- An **iPhone** fast charges from a **USB-C PD** port with a USB-C to Lightning or USB-C to USB-C cable.
- Many **Android phones** use PD on USB-C or Quick Charge on USB-A. Check your phone's spec.

The GP-CA02 has a USB-A port with Quick Charge 3.0 and a USB-C port with PD, and comes with a USB-C to USB-C cable. Check the rating printed for the port you will use.

# Step 4: cables that reach

In a car, cable length matters. A 1 m cable may not reach the back seat.

# On the road

- Plug the charger in after starting the engine.
- Keep the phone out of direct sun on the dashboard. A shaded mount or the cup holder is better.
- Download offline maps before you leave, in case of weak signal.
- Unplug when you park for a long stop, so you do not drain the car battery.
