When an iPhone's battery drains fast, start with Settings, then Battery. The usage list shows which apps used your charge and whether they did it on screen or in the background. Most fast drain comes from one app, a bright screen, location services, background refresh or a weak signal, and you can fix those in a few minutes. If none of these explains it, check Battery Health to see whether the battery is worn.

Menu names change slightly between iOS versions. If you cannot find something, swipe down on the Settings screen and use the search bar.

# 1. Battery usage

Open **Settings**, then **Battery**, and scroll to the list of apps.

- Tap **Show Activity** (or the time view) to see on-screen and background time for each app.
- An app with a lot of background activity but little screen time is a likely culprit.
- iOS sometimes shows **Battery Suggestions** at the top. They are worth reading.

# 2. Display

- **Brightness:** open **Settings**, then **Accessibility**, then **Display & Text Size**, and make sure **Auto-Brightness** is on.
- **Auto-Lock:** under **Settings**, then **Display & Brightness**, set Auto-Lock to 30 seconds or one minute.
- **Dark mode:** on iPhones with OLED screens, dark mode can save some power.
- **Always On:** on models that have an always-on display, turning it off saves battery.

# 3. Location services

Open **Settings**, then **Privacy & Security**, then **Location Services**.

- Go through the list and set apps to **While Using** or **Never** where they do not need your location all the time.
- Under **System Services** at the bottom, you can turn off items you do not need. Leave **Find My iPhone** on.

# 4. Background App Refresh

Open **Settings**, then **General**, then **Background App Refresh**.

- Turn it off for apps that do not need to update in the background.
- You can also set it to work only on Wi-Fi.
- Leave it on for messaging apps you rely on.

# 5. Notifications

Every notification can wake the screen. Under **Settings**, then **Notifications**, turn off notifications from apps you do not need to hear from, or use a Focus mode during busy times.

# 6. Network and signal

- **Weak signal:** an iPhone on poor coverage works harder to stay connected. If you will be somewhere with no signal for hours, turn on **Airplane Mode**.
- **Wi-Fi:** use reliable Wi-Fi where you have it.
- **5G:** on 5G models, under **Settings**, then **Mobile Data** (or Cellular), then **Mobile Data Options**, then **Voice & Data**, **5G Auto** uses 5G only when it is worth it. Choosing **LTE** can save battery where 5G coverage is patchy.
- **Personal Hotspot:** turn it off when you finish.

# 7. Software and Low Power Mode

- **Restart** the iPhone.
- **Update iOS** under **Settings**, then **General**, then **Software Update**. After a big update, the phone may use more battery for a day or two while it re-indexes photos and files.
- **Low Power Mode:** under **Settings**, then **Battery**. Use it to stretch the last part of the day, during a power cut or on a long trip upcountry.

# 8. Check Battery Health

Open **Settings**, then **Battery**, then **Battery Health** (on some iOS versions, **Battery Health & Charging**).

- A low **Maximum Capacity** means the battery holds less than when new.
- A message that **performance management** has been applied, or that a service is recommended, means the battery is the problem.
- If the back or screen is lifting, the battery is swollen: stop using and charging the phone straight away.
