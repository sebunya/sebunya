"5V 2A" means the charger gives 5 volts at up to 2 amps. Multiply them and you get 10 watts. That is the standard, non-fast charging level for many phones. A fast charger lists more than one line, such as 5V⎓2A and 9V⎓2A, and uses the higher one only when the phone asks for it.

Our guide on charger watts covers the sum itself. This one walks through everything else on the label.

# The two main lines: input and output

- **Input** is what the charger takes from the wall socket.
- **Output** is what it gives your phone through the USB port.

People often mix them up. The input line has the big voltage (from the mains). The output line has the small voltage your phone uses.

# Reading the input line

A typical input line looks like this: **100-240V ~50/60Hz 0.3A.**

- **100-240V** is the range of mains voltage the charger accepts. A range this wide covers mains in most countries, including Uganda.
- **~** means alternating current (AC), which is what comes from the wall.
- **50/60Hz** is the frequency of the mains. Chargers that list both work on either.
- **0.3A** is the most current the charger draws from the wall. It is much smaller than the output current, because the mains voltage is much higher.

# Reading the output line

A typical output line: **5V⎓2A.**

- **5V** is the voltage. Standard USB is 5V.
- **⎓** (a solid line over a dashed line) means direct current (DC), which is what a phone uses.
- **2A** is the most current the charger can give at that voltage.
- **Watts** = 5 × 2 = 10W.

The phone draws the current it needs, up to that limit. A 2A charger does not force 2A into a phone that wants less.

# When there are several output lines

Fast chargers print a line for each voltage they support:

- **5V⎓2A** = 10W
- **9V⎓1A** = 9W, or 9V⎓2A = 18W
- **12V⎓1.5A** = 18W

Each line is a separate profile. The phone and charger agree on one, using a standard such as USB PD or Quick Charge. If they share no standard, they stay on the 5V line.

For example, our [GP-C08 charger](/products/goldplus-charger-gp-c08) lists 5V⎓2A and 9V⎓1A, so the most it lists is 10W.

# Lines for each port and for both together

On a charger with two ports you may see:

- A list for **USB-C** (sometimes marked "Type-C" or "C").
- A list for **USB-A** (sometimes marked "A" or "USB").
- A **total** or **C + A** line for both in use.

Do not add the single-port figures together. When both ports are used, the total line applies. Our [GP-C11 dual-port charger](/products/goldplus-charger-gp-c11) is listed at 30W with a USB-A and a USB-C port; read its label for what each port gives alone and together.

# Other marks you may see

- **PD** or **PPS:** USB Power Delivery, the USB-C fast-charging standard, and its programmable mode.
- **QC** or **Quick Charge:** Qualcomm's fast-charging standard, often on a USB-A port.
- **A square inside a square:** double insulation. The charger does not need an earth connection, which is why its top pin may be plastic.
- **Model number and maker's details:** a genuine charger names its model and its maker.

Some labels also carry safety or approval marks. These can be copied by fakes, so treat them as one clue among many, not proof.

# Cable numbers

Cables have numbers too. A cable marked **3A** can carry up to 3 amps. That describes current, not data speed: a 3A cable is not automatically a USB 3.0 data cable. Our [GP-L03C USB-A to USB-C cable](/products/goldplus-cable-gp-l03c) is rated 3A.

# Use the label when you shop

- Compare chargers by the output lines on the label, not the number on the box.
- Check the line for the port you will use.
- If the box and the label disagree, trust the label, and ask us to read the exact unit.
