If your phone has a USB-C port, both cables will charge it. The choice depends on the port on your charger. **USB-C to USB-C** fits chargers with a USB-C port, and is the usual way to get USB Power Delivery (PD) fast charging. **USB-A to USB-C** fits the larger rectangular USB-A port found on older chargers, computers, car chargers and many power banks.

# Look at your charger first

- **USB-A port:** a flat rectangle, about 12 mm wide. You need a USB-A to USB-C cable.
- **USB-C port:** a small oval. You need a USB-C to USB-C cable.
- **Both ports:** many chargers and power banks have one of each. Use the USB-C port with a USB-C to USB-C cable for the fastest PD charging, if your phone supports it.

A cable that does not fit the charger is no use however good it is, so check this before anything else.

# Fast charging differences

## USB-C to USB-C

USB Power Delivery runs over USB-C at both ends. With a PD charger and a phone that supports PD, this is the cable that unlocks it. Many Android phones support PD, and Samsung's Super Fast Charging uses a version of PD called PPS. USB-C to USB-C cables are usually rated in watts, for example 20W, 60W or higher.

The same cable can often charge a tablet, and many laptops that charge over USB-C, if its rating is high enough for them.

## USB-A to USB-C

USB-A ports do not carry USB Power Delivery. A USB-A to USB-C cable can still fast charge with systems that work over USB-A, such as Qualcomm Quick Charge, or a phone maker's own fast-charging system, when the charger and phone both support it. Otherwise it charges at standard speed.

USB-A to USB-C cables are usually rated in amps, such as 3A or 6A.

# Data and computers

Both kinds can move files between a phone and a computer, if the cable carries data. Many computers still have only USB-A ports, so a USB-A to USB-C cable is often the one that fits. Newer laptops may have only USB-C ports.

# When to own both

Many households end up with both, and that is sensible:

- A **USB-C to USB-C** cable for the fast charger at home.
- A **USB-A to USB-C** cable for the car, the office computer, or an older charger or power bank.
