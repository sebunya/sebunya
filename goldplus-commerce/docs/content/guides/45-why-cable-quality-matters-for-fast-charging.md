The cable is part of the charging system, not just a wire between charger and phone. A thin, damaged or under-rated cable can quietly turn a fast charger into a slow one. If your phone charges slowly even on a good charger, the cable is the first thing to check.

# What the cable actually does

Power flows from the charger, through the cable, into the phone. Every cable has some electrical resistance, and the more current it carries, the more that resistance matters.

- **Thin wires** have more resistance. Some power is lost as heat, and the voltage that reaches the phone drops.
- **When the phone sees the voltage drop,** it may pull back the charging speed to stay safe.
- **For fast charging over USB-C,** the cable also carries the signals that let the phone and charger agree on a faster mode. If those wires are missing or damaged, the two may fall back to basic charging.

The result is the same either way: the phone charges at normal speed, or slower, even though the charger could do more.

# What a fast-charging cable needs

- **The right plugs at both ends.** For USB Power Delivery (PD), that usually means USB-C at the charger end: USB-C to USB-C for most Android phones, USB-C to Lightning for Lightning iPhones.
- **A rating at least as high as the charging you want.** Most USB-C cables are built for up to 60W. Above that, for example a 100W laptop charger, the cable needs a higher rating. A cable's printed watt or amp rating tells you the most it is built to carry.
- **Solid connectors.** The metal plug and the joint where it meets the cable take the most strain. Reinforced plugs and a strain-relief collar help them survive daily use.

A higher-rated cable never forces extra power into your phone. The phone and charger still agree on the speed between them; the cable just stops being the bottleneck.

# Signs your cable is slowing you down

- The "fast charging" message on your phone has stopped appearing with the same charger.
- Charging works only when the cable is held at a certain angle.
- The plug or the cable near the plug gets warm.
- The outer jacket is split, kinked or showing wire.
- Charging is faster with a different cable on the same charger.

The last one is the simplest test. Swap in a cable you trust, keep the same charger and the same socket, and compare.

> A cable that is frayed, exposes wire, or gets hot should be thrown away, not taped up. Heat at the plug is a warning sign.

# Why cheap cables are often the problem

A cable can look perfect from the outside and still have thin wires inside. Fake or low-grade cables commonly cut costs on the copper and on the connectors. They may charge a phone, but slowly, and they tend to fail at the plug after a short time.

You cannot see inside a cable, so buy from a seller who states what the cable is rated for, and check the listing for the connectors, the length and the rating before you pay.
