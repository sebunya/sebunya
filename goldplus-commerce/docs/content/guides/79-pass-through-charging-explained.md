Pass-through charging means a power bank can charge your phone while the power bank itself is plugged into the wall and charging. It turns the power bank into a kind of hub: one socket fills both. Not every power bank supports it, and some that do behave differently from others, so check the manual for your exact model before you rely on it.

# How it works

Without pass-through, a power bank does one job at a time: either it takes power in, or it gives power out. When it is plugged in, its outputs may switch off.

With pass-through, the power coming in from the charger is shared. Some goes to the phone, and some goes into the power bank's own cells. When the phone is full, more goes into the power bank. The exact way the power is split depends on the design.

# When it is useful

- **One socket, two things to charge.** In a hostel, office or guest house with few free sockets, one charger can fill the phone and the power bank overnight.
- **Power cuts.** If the power returns for a short while, you can start filling both at once, and the phone keeps charging from the power bank if the power drops again.
- **Travel.** At a busy airport or bus park socket, one plug does two jobs.

# The trade-offs

**It is slower.** The incoming power is shared, so both the phone and the power bank take longer to fill than they would on their own.

**It makes more heat.** Charging and discharging at the same time means more work for the electronics and the cells, and more warmth. Heat is the main thing that ages lithium cells.

**It may age the power bank faster** if done all the time, because of that heat and constant activity. Using it now and then is different from using a power bank as a permanent desk charger.

**Behaviour varies.** Some power banks briefly stop output when the charger is plugged in or removed, which can interrupt charging. Some prioritise the phone, others the power bank.

# How to check if yours supports it

1. **Read the manual or box.** Look for "pass-through" or a statement that the power bank can charge devices while charging.
2. **Do not guess from the ports.** Having an input and an output does not mean both can be used at once.
3. **Test carefully if the manual is silent.** Plug the power bank into the charger, then connect your phone, and see if the phone shows it is charging. Watch for heat. If the manual says not to, do not.
4. **Ask before you buy.** If pass-through matters to you, ask us about the exact model. We will check its documentation rather than guess.

# Safe habits if you use it

- Use a good charger and a cable in good condition.
- Keep everything on a hard, open surface, never on a bed or under a pillow.
- Stop if the power bank gets hot, swells or smells of burning.
- Unplug once both are full rather than leaving them connected all night, every night.

# The simpler alternative

If you have a charger with two ports, you can charge the phone and the power bank side by side instead. Each gets its own port, less heat builds up in the power bank, and nothing depends on pass-through support. Read the charger's label for what the ports give together.
