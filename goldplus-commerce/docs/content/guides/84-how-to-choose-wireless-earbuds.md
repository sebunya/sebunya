The best wireless earbuds for you are the ones that fit the way you live. Start with three questions: how do you want to wear them, what will you mostly do with them, and how long do they need to last between charges? Answer those and the choice gets much smaller.

# Step 1: decide how you want to wear them

There are two main styles.

- **In-ear earbuds** sit inside the ear opening. They block some outside sound just by being there, which helps on a noisy taxi or in a busy market.
- **Open-ear earphones** rest outside the ear canal, held by a clip or a hook. You still hear traffic, colleagues and announcements around you.

If you walk along busy roads, ride as a boda passenger or need to hear people at work, open-ear can be the safer and more comfortable choice. If you want your music to drown out the room, in-ear suits you better.

# Step 2: think about what you will use them for

**Mostly music and videos.** Look at play time (sometimes called music time) and how the listing describes the sound. Some listings state a frequency response or mention bass enhancement.

**Mostly calls.** Look for a talk time figure, a microphone, and any call feature the listing names, such as HD call or noise reduction. Our GP03BT lists 5 hours of talk time, and the GP-W04 lists 4 to 5 hours.

**Long days away from a socket.** The charging case matters as much as the earbuds. The case recharges the earbuds when you put them away, so a full case keeps you going through a long day or an upcountry trip.

# Step 3: read the battery figures properly

Earbud listings often give several numbers:

- **Play time or music time:** how long the earbuds play on one charge.
- **Talk time:** how long they last on calls.
- **Standby time:** how long they keep their charge while connected but silent.
- **Charging time:** how long a full recharge takes.

Check the conditions too. Our GP02BT and GP03BT state their music time at 80% volume. Louder listening usually means shorter play time, so treat these figures as a guide, not a promise for every use.

# Step 4: check the small things that matter every day

- **Charging port.** A Type-C port means you can often use the same cable as your phone. The GP-W03, GP-W05 and GP-W06 all list a Type-C port.
- **Battery display.** An LED display on the case shows how much charge is left, so you are not caught out. The GP-W03 and GP02BT list one.
- **Controls.** Smart touch lets you change tracks and volume by tapping the earbud. The GP-W04 and GP-W05 list it.
- **Bluetooth version.** Most of our current earbuds list Bluetooth 5.4. It is useful to know, but the fit, battery and calls usually matter more day to day.
- **Range.** Our listings state 10 m. Walls and bodies shorten that in practice, so keep the phone reasonably close.
