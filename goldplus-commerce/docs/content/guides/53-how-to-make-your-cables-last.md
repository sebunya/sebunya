Most charging cables fail in the same place: the joint where the plug meets the cable. Sharp bends, pulling by the cord, and heat do almost all the damage. Change a few habits and the same cable can last much longer.

# What wears a cable out

- **Bending at the plug.** Using the phone while it charges, with the cable folded at a right angle, strains the joint every minute.
- **Pulling by the cord.** Yanking the cable out of the wall or the phone puts the full force on the thin wires inside.
- **Tight wrapping.** Winding a cable tightly around a charger or your fingers kinks it and stresses both ends.
- **Heat.** A cable left on a car dashboard in the sun, or under a pillow while charging, ages faster.
- **Crushing.** Doors, chair legs, and the edge of a bed frame flatten and break wires.
- **Dirt and moisture** in the plug or the phone port cause poor contact and heat.

# Habits that help

1. **Hold the plug, not the cable,** when you unplug from the phone or the charger.
2. **Keep the cable straight at the plug** while you use the phone. A longer cable makes this easier in bed or on the sofa.
3. **Coil loosely.** Make loose loops about the size of your palm, and secure them with a soft tie or band.
4. **Do not wrap it round the charger** while the charger is still warm.
5. **Keep it off the floor** where it can be stepped on, or run it along the wall.
6. **Keep plugs clean and dry.** Store cables in a bag pocket, not loose in the bottom with sand and dust.
7. **Unplug the charger** from the wall when you are done, rather than leaving the cable dangling.

# Storing cables for travel

On an upcountry trip or a long boda ride, cables get crushed and tangled in bags. Coil each one loosely, keep it in its own small pouch or bag pocket, and keep it away from keys and coins that can get into the plug.

Flat cables tangle less in a bag. Braided cables resist the rubbing of zips and buckles.

# Choosing a cable that lasts

How you treat a cable matters most, but build helps too:

- **Reinforced plugs** with a flexible collar spread the bending over a longer section.
- **A braided jacket** resists kinks, scuffs and tangles.
- **The right length** for where you charge means less stretching and bending.

# When to replace a cable

Replace the cable straight away if:

- The outer jacket is split and you can see wire or foil.
- The plug or cable gets hot while charging.
- The plug's metal is blackened, burnt or bent.
- Charging only works at a certain angle.

> Do not tape up a damaged cable. Tape hides the damage but does not stop heat building at a broken wire. A new cable costs far less than a damaged phone port.
