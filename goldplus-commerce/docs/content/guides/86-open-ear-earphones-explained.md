Open-ear earphones suit people who want to listen without shutting out the world. They rest on or around the outer ear instead of sitting inside the ear canal, so you still hear traffic, a colleague calling your name or a conductor shouting the stage. The trade-off is that the world also hears a little of your music, and loud places can drown it out.

# How open-ear earphones work

Normal earbuds push a small speaker into the ear opening and seal it. Open-ear designs leave the opening clear. The speaker sits just outside it and points the sound in.

They stay in place in one of two ways:

- **A clip** that hooks over the edge of the ear, like the [GP-W04](/products/goldplus-bluetooth-gp-w04), which its listing describes as an over-the-ear clip, open style.
- **An ear hook** that curves over the top of the ear, like the [GP-W06](/products/goldplus-bluetooth-gp-w06) and the [GP02BT](/products/goldplus-bluetooth-gp02bt). The GP02BT's listing says its hook is memory steel.

# Who they suit

**People on the move in town.** If you walk along busy Kampala roads or ride as a boda passenger, hearing horns and engines matters. Open-ear lets you keep music or a call going while staying aware.

**People at work.** Shop attendants, receptionists and anyone who needs to hear customers can keep one ear on a call and still respond to the room.

**People who find earbuds uncomfortable.** Some people dislike the pressure of something inside the ear, or find earbuds keep falling out. Open-ear designs avoid both.

**Long wear.** Because nothing seals the ear, many people find them easier to wear for hours.

# Who they may not suit

- **Noisy places.** On a packed taxi or next to a generator, outside noise competes with your audio. You may be tempted to turn the volume right up, which is not good for your ears.
- **Private listening.** At high volume, people near you may hear some of what you play.
- **Deep bass lovers.** Without a seal, bass usually feels lighter than with in-ear earbuds.

# Clip or hook?

- **Clip:** compact and quick to put on. Good if you wear glasses or a cap, since there is less over the top of the ear.
- **Hook:** wraps over the ear, which many people find steadier when walking or moving around.

# A note on safety

Hearing what is around you is the point of open-ear, so keep the volume moderate enough that you still can. If you cannot hear traffic, the music is too loud.
