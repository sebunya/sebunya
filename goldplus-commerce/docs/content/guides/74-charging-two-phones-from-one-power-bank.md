Yes, many power banks can charge two phones at the same time. The catch is that the power bank's output is usually shared between them, so each phone charges more slowly than it would alone, and fast charging may switch off. If one phone needs charge quickly, charge it first on its own.

# How shared output works

A power bank has a limit on the total power it can give at once. When one phone is connected, it can have the full amount the port supports. When two are connected, that total is divided between them.

Many power banks print this on the label: a figure for each port, and a separate figure for all ports together, sometimes marked "Total output". Read the combined figure. Do not add the per-port figures together; the total is usually lower than their sum.

Some power banks also drop out of fast charging when more than one device is connected, and fall back to standard speed for both. The label or manual tells you how your model behaves.

# Built-in cables and extra ports

A power bank with several built-in cables or several ports looks ready to charge many phones at once. Whether all the outputs can be used at the same time, and how the power is shared, differs from model to model. Check the manual for the exact unit, or ask us before you rely on it.

For example, the [GP-P02A](/products/goldplus-power-bank-gp-p02a-pb) and [GP-P02W](/products/goldplus-power-bank-gp-p02w-pb) each list four built-in cables (Lightning, USB-C, Micro-USB and USB-A), which helps when the two phones have different connectors.

# Plan the energy

Two phones empty a power bank twice as fast. A compact 10,000mAh power bank may give each phone only a partial charge. If you often share, a 20,000mAh unit such as the [GP-P07](/products/goldplus-power-bank-gp-p07-pb), GP-P02A or GP-P02W gives more room.

Remember that no capacity figure promises an exact number of charges. Energy is lost as heat in the power bank, the cable and the phone.

# When to charge one at a time

- **One phone is nearly flat and needed now.** Give it the full output first.
- **You want fast charging.** Many power banks only fast charge one device at a time.
- **The power bank is low.** Prioritise the phone that matters most, rather than splitting the last charge.
- **It is getting hot.** Two devices mean more heat. Unplug one and let it cool.

# Tips for sharing

1. Agree a top-up level, such as 50%, so both phones get something.
2. Swap after a set time rather than letting one phone fill completely.
3. Keep both phones and the power bank on a hard surface, out of the sun.
4. Avoid heavy use of either phone while it charges.
5. Use cables in good condition. A worn cable on one phone wastes energy that the other phone could have had.

# Different phones, different speeds

Two phones on the same power bank rarely charge at the same rate. A phone that supports fast charging may take a bigger share, while an older phone charges at standard speed. A phone that is nearly full also draws less power than one that is nearly empty. That is normal. If the slower phone matters more, unplug the other one for a while.

# Choose a power bank for two

Look for a 20,000mAh size, connectors that match both phones, and a label that shows the combined output. A digital charge display helps you see how much is left for the second phone.
