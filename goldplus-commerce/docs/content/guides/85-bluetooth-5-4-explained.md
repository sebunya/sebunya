Bluetooth 5.4 on an earbud listing tells you the earbuds use a recent version of the Bluetooth standard. That is a good sign, but it is not a promise of better sound, longer battery or a stronger connection on its own. Those depend far more on the earbuds' design and on your phone.

Here is what the number does and does not mean.

# What a Bluetooth version is

Bluetooth is a shared standard, managed by an industry group, that lets devices from different makers talk to each other wirelessly. Every few years a new version is published, numbered 5.0, 5.1, 5.2, 5.3, 5.4 and so on. Each version adds options that device makers can choose to use.

The important word is *options*. A new version makes features possible. It does not force every device to use all of them.

# What Bluetooth 5.4 added

Bluetooth 5.4 was published in 2023. Most of what it added is aimed at low-energy devices that send small amounts of data, such as electronic price labels in shops and sensors, rather than at music.

So for earbuds, moving from 5.3 to 5.4 is a small step, not a leap. Seeing 5.4 mainly tells you the earbuds are built around a recent chip.

# Will it work with my older phone?

Yes, in normal use. Bluetooth versions are designed to work with earlier ones, so Bluetooth 5.4 earbuds will pair with a phone that has Bluetooth 5.0, 5.2 or similar.

When two devices with different versions connect, they use the features both of them support. In practice that means the older device sets the limit. Your earbuds will play music and take calls; they simply will not use newer features your phone does not have.

# What actually decides how good earbuds feel

If you are comparing two pairs, these usually matter more than the Bluetooth number:

- **Fit and style.** In-ear or open-ear, and whether they stay put while you move.
- **Battery figures.** Play time, talk time and what the charging case adds.
- **Calls.** A microphone and any call feature the listing names.
- **Controls and case.** Touch controls, a battery display, the charging port.
- **Your phone.** Its own Bluetooth hardware, settings and software all affect the connection.

# What about range?

Range is how far the phone can be from the earbuds before the sound breaks up. Our earbud listings state a range of 10 m. Like all wireless figures, that is measured in good conditions. Walls, your own body and a phone deep in a bag all shorten it.

If music cuts out when the phone is in your back pocket, try moving it to a front pocket or a bag on the same side as the earbuds.
