The key to staying charged on a long trip is to leave Kampala with everything full, carry a large power bank with the right cables, and treat your phone battery as something to ration on the road. Where you are going, power may be less reliable, so plan to recharge whenever you get the chance.

# Before you leave

**Charge everything the night before.** Phone, power bank, earbuds and anything else that runs on a battery.

**Take a big enough power bank.** For a trip of several days, a 20,000mAh power bank gives far more room than a compact one. Our [GP-P07](/products/goldplus-power-bank-gp-p07-pb) and [GP-P02W](/products/goldplus-power-bank-gp-p02w-pb) are both listed at 20,000mAh.

**Pack the right cables.** Check every device's port: USB-C, Lightning or Micro-USB. A power bank with built-in cables saves you from leaving a lead behind; a spare separate cable is still a good idea.

**Pack the charger.** Your wall charger lets you refill phone and power bank wherever you find a socket.

**Download what you need.** Maps, music and documents downloaded at home use less battery and data on the road, especially where the signal is weak.

# On the road

Weak signal makes a phone work harder, and that drains the battery. On a long bus or taxi ride:

- Turn on battery saver early, not when you reach 10%.
- Lower screen brightness.
- Use offline maps, and turn on navigation only when you need it.
- Switch on airplane mode during long stretches with no signal, if you do not need to be reached.
- Top up in short bursts from the power bank, keeping some in reserve.

If you are driving, a car charger in the 12V socket keeps the phone going while the power bank stays full for later. See our [car accessories](/car-accessories).

# When you arrive

Power at your destination may be patchy or switched off in the evenings.

- **Charge whenever you can.** A guest house socket or a shop that offers charging is a chance to refill the power bank, not just the phone.
- **Charge the power bank first** if you have only a short time at a socket and the phone still has some charge.
- **Use a lamp, not your phone torch,** for evenings without power. The [GP-P02W](/products/goldplus-power-bank-gp-p02w-pb) and [GP-P03A](/products/goldplus-power-bank-gp-p03a-pb) both list a camping lamp.
- **Keep it cool.** Do not leave power banks or phones in a parked car or in direct sun.

# A simple packing list

1. Phone and wall charger.
2. A 20,000mAh power bank, fully charged.
3. Cables for every device, even if the power bank has some built in.
4. Car charger, if you are driving.
5. A small pouch to keep it all dry and dust-free.

# Safety on long journeys

- Do not charge a power bank on a seat in direct sun or under a pile of bags.
- If a power bank gets very hot, swells or smells of burning, unplug it and stop using it.
- Keep it in a closed bag in rain and dust.
