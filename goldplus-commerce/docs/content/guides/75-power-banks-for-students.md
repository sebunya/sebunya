# Why campus days drain phones

Lecture notes, group chats, online portals, mobile money, maps and the occasional video all use the battery. Sockets in lecture rooms and libraries are often taken or not working, and hostel power can be cut at the wrong moment. A power bank turns that worry into a non-issue.

# What to look for

**A size you will actually carry.** A power bank left in the hostel helps nobody. A palm-sized unit such as the [GP-P06](/products/goldplus-power-bank-gp-p06-pb), listed as 10,000mAh and fitting a pocket, suits most students.

**The cable built in.** Leads get lent out and never come back. Built-in cables mean your power bank always has its cable. The GP-P06 has USB-C and Lightning cables built in.

**Fast charging between classes.** If your phone supports USB Power Delivery (PD), a PD power bank tops it up faster in a short break. The GP-P06 lists 22.5W with PD.

**A charge display.** Knowing the power bank's level helps you remember to recharge it the night before an exam day.

# Hostel safety

Hostels and shared rooms bring their own risks:

- **Never charge a power bank on your bed or under your pillow.** Charge it on a desk or the floor, on a hard surface.
- **Do not overload extension cables** with many chargers at once.
- **Unplug once it is full** rather than charging all night, every night.
- **Stop using it if it swells,** gets very hot or smells of burning.

# Keep it from walking off

Power banks are easy to borrow and forget to return.

- Mark it with your name or a sticker.
- Keep it in an inside pocket of your bag, not on the desk when you step out.
- Lend it with the phone in front of you, not overnight.

# Look after it

- Keep it out of hot sun at outdoor events and sports days.
- Recharge it when the power is on, so it is ready when the power is not.
- Before the long holidays, leave it about half charged if you will not use it.

# Exam season tip

Charge both phone and power bank the night before an important day, and pack the power bank with your ID and pens. One less thing to worry about.
