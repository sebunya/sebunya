If your iPhone is an iPhone 5 up to an iPhone 14 model, it charges through a **Lightning** port. For fast charging, use a **USB-C to Lightning** cable with a USB-C Power Delivery (PD) charger. If your iPhone is an iPhone 15 or newer, it has a USB-C port and needs a USB-C cable instead.

# Step 1: check which iPhone you have

Open Settings, then General, then About. The model name is listed there.

- **iPhone 5 to iPhone 14 series,** including the iPhone SE (2nd and 3rd generation): Lightning.
- **iPhone 15 series and newer:** USB-C. Skip Lightning and see our [USB-C cables](/power/charging-cables).

Some iPads and older AirPods cases also use Lightning, so the same cable can serve them.

# Step 2: choose the charger end

A Lightning cable has Lightning at the phone end and either USB-A or USB-C at the other.

- **USB-C to Lightning** is the one for fast charging. iPhones from the iPhone 8 onwards fast charge with a USB-C PD charger of about 20W.
- **USB-A to Lightning** fits older chargers, computers and car chargers. It charges at normal speed.

If you already have a USB-C PD charger, a USB-C to Lightning cable is the upgrade that makes the difference.

# Step 3: check the rating and build

- **A watt rating** on a USB-C to Lightning cable tells you the most power it is built to carry. A cable rated above 20W gives headroom; the iPhone still only takes what it can use.
- **Flat or braided cables** resist tangles and kinks.
- **The plug joint** is where Lightning cables usually fail. Pull from the plug, not the cable.

# Signs of trouble

- **"This accessory may not be supported"** can appear when a cable is damaged, the plug or port is dirty, or the cable is not compatible. Try another cable to see which it is.
- **Charging stops and starts** when you move the phone.
- **The plug feels loose** in the port.

Often the cause is pocket lint packed into the iPhone's port. Switch the phone off and gently remove it with a wooden toothpick. Never use metal, and never use water. If the port still fails, have it looked at.

> If the metal contacts on a Lightning plug are blackened or burnt, stop using that cable.
