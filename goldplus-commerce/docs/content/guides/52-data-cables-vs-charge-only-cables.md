When a phone charges from a computer but the computer does not show the phone's files, there are two likely causes. Either the cable is **charge-only** and has no data wires, or the phone is connected but has not been told to share its files. Check the phone first, then try a cable you know carries data.

# What makes a cable charge-only

A USB cable has separate wires for power and for data. A charge-only cable has the power wires and leaves the data wires out. It charges normally, but to the computer it looks like nothing is connected.

Charge-only cables are common with cheap gadgets, some power banks, and cables sold for charging alone. They look exactly like data cables from the outside, so the packaging or listing is the only way to know before you try.

A data cable that has been bent and pulled for months can also lose its data connection while still charging. The data wires are thin and tend to break first.

# Step 1: check the phone

## Android

1. Connect the phone and unlock it.
2. Pull down the notification shade. Look for a message such as "Charging this device via USB" or "USB options".
3. Tap it and choose **File transfer** (sometimes called MTP or "Transfer files").
4. The phone should now appear on the computer.

If no USB message appears at all, the cable is probably not carrying data.

On a Mac, an Android phone's files do not show up on their own; an extra file-transfer app is needed.

## iPhone

1. Connect the iPhone and unlock it.
2. If it asks **"Trust This Computer?"**, tap Trust and enter your passcode.
3. On a Windows computer, Apple's own software may need to be installed before the iPhone's photos appear.

If the trust message never appears, try another cable.

# Step 2: test the cable

- **Swap cables.** Use a cable you know has moved files before. If that one works, the first cable is charge-only or its data wires are damaged.
- **Swap USB ports** on the computer, and avoid unpowered hubs and front-panel ports that may be loose.
- **Restart both** the phone and the computer if nothing changes.

> Keep your data cable labelled or in a different colour, so it does not get mixed up with charge-only cables around the house.

# Charging speed is a separate question

A computer's USB port usually gives less power than a wall charger, so a phone connected to a computer often charges slowly. That is normal and has nothing to do with whether the cable carries data.

Also, a cable's amp rating, like 3A, is about current, not data. A cable marked 3A does not, by itself, mean USB 3.0 data speed.

# Where data cables matter

- Backing up photos and videos to a laptop or flash drive.
- Copying music or documents to a phone.
- Moving files at an office or internet café where Wi-Fi is slow.
- Updating or repairing some phones with a computer.
