When you camp, hike or spend a long day at an outdoor event, a power bank is your only reliable source of charge. Plan for it before you go: start with everything full, take a large power bank, protect it from sun, rain and dust, and use your phone sparingly. A power bank with a built-in lamp does double duty after dark.

# Plan how much power you need

Think about how many days you will be away from a socket and how many devices you will charge. As a rough guide:

- **A day out:** a compact 10,000mAh power bank for one phone.
- **A night or two camping, or two phones:** a 20,000mAh power bank.
- **Longer trips or a group:** more than one power bank, or plan a stop where you can recharge.

No capacity figure promises an exact number of phone charges, because energy is lost as heat along the way. Allow some margin.

# Why a lamp helps outdoors

After dark at a campsite, you need light for cooking, finding things in the tent and walking to the toilet. Using your phone torch drains the battery you need for calls and photos. A power bank with a built-in lamp keeps the phone free.

# Protect it from the outdoors

**Sun.** Never leave a power bank on a rock, a car roof or a tent in direct sun. Heat ages the cells and can be dangerous. Keep it in the shade, inside a bag.

**Rain and dew.** Keep it in a closed bag or a plastic pouch, especially overnight. Do not charge or use a wet power bank; let it dry fully first.

**Dust and sand.** Keep the ports and cables clean. Tuck built-in cables into their slots and store the power bank in a pouch.

**Cold nights.** Lithium-ion cells give less in the cold. Keep the power bank in your sleeping bag or a jacket pocket overnight, but never charge it while it is very cold.

# Make every bit of charge count

1. Turn on battery saver before you leave.
2. Use airplane mode when there is no signal; searching for a network drains the battery.
3. Lower screen brightness and close apps you are not using.
4. Download maps before you go and turn on navigation only when needed.
5. Top up in short bursts, keeping some charge in reserve for emergencies.

# Tips for groups

- Agree who carries which power bank and who charges when.
- Label cables and power banks so they come home with the right person.
- Keep one power bank as a reserve for emergencies only.

# Safety outdoors

- Keep power banks away from campfires, stoves and fuel.
- Do not leave one charging in a closed tent in the sun.
- Stop using any power bank that gets very hot, swells or smells of burning.
