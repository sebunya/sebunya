"Noise reduction" can mean two very different things. One kind cleans up your voice so the person you are calling hears less of the street. The other reduces the noise *you* hear while you listen. A listing that only says "noise reduction" does not tell you which, so read it carefully and ask if it matters to you.

# The three ways earphones deal with noise

## Passive isolation

This is simply the earbud blocking your ear. In-ear earbuds that sit snugly in the ear opening stop some outside sound getting in, with no electronics involved. A good fit matters more here than anything else.

Open-ear earphones do not block the ear at all, by design. That is their point: you still hear what is around you.

## ENC: environmental noise cancellation

ENC is about calls. The earbud's microphones pick up your voice and the background noise, and processing reduces the background before your voice is sent. The person on the other end hears you more clearly over traffic, wind or a busy shop.

ENC does not make your music quieter or your surroundings silent. It works on the sound going *out* of your earbuds, not the sound coming in.

## ANC: active noise cancelling

ANC is about listening. Microphones on the earbud hear steady outside noise, such as an engine hum or a generator, and the earbud plays a matching "anti-noise" signal that cancels some of it in your ear. You hear your music with less background.

ANC is usually named clearly as "ANC" or "active noise cancelling" on a listing, because it is a selling point. If a listing does not say ANC, do not assume it is there.

# How to read any listing

- **"ANC" or "active noise cancelling":** reduces what you hear. Check whether it can be switched on and off.
- **"ENC", "call noise reduction" or "clear calls":** usually about your voice on calls.
- **"Noise reduction" on its own:** could be either. Ask.
- **"Noise isolating":** usually means passive isolation from the fit.

Be wary of any product that claims to block "all" noise. No earbuds do that.

# Which do you actually need?

- **You take lots of calls in noisy places:** look for call noise reduction (ENC) and a good microphone.
- **You want quiet on long journeys:** look for a listing that clearly says ANC, and a snug in-ear fit.
- **You need to hear traffic and people:** choose open-ear and keep the volume moderate.

Remember that with any earphones, turning the volume up to beat background noise is hard on your ears. A better fit or a quieter spot helps more.
