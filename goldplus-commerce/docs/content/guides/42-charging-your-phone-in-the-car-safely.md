Charging a phone in the car is safe when you follow a few simple rules: use a well-made car charger, plug it in after the engine has started, keep the phone out of direct sun, and keep the cable where it cannot tangle with the driver. Most problems come from heat, loose cables and cheap chargers.

# Plug in after starting the engine

When the engine starts, the car's voltage can dip and then jump for a moment. Plugging the phone in after the engine is running avoids exposing it to that.

When you switch off at the end of a trip, unplug the phone before you leave the car.

# Heat is the biggest risk

Inside a parked car in the Kampala sun, the temperature rises fast. Phones and lithium-ion batteries do not like heat.

- **Do not leave a phone charging on the dashboard in direct sun.** It will slow down or stop charging to protect itself, and long exposure to heat wears the battery.
- **Use a shaded spot** such as the cup holder or a vent-side holder away from the windscreen.
- **Never leave a phone or power bank in a parked car** in the sun.
- **Unplug if the phone gets very hot.** Let it cool before charging again.

# Keep the cable out of the way

A cable can be a hazard to the driver.

- **Route it away from the gear lever, pedals and steering wheel.**
- **Do not let it hang across the driver's legs.**
- **Use a length that fits.** Too short and it pulls on the port; too long and it tangles. A 1 m cable suits the front seats; a longer one is for the back.
- **Do not trap it in doors or seat rails,** which damages the wires inside.

# Drive first, phone second

Set up your map and music before you set off. If you need to change something on the way, ask a passenger or pull over safely. Plugging in a cable while driving takes your eyes off the road.

# Look after the socket

- **Keep it clean.** Coins, crumbs and dust in the socket can cause a poor connection or even blow the fuse.
- **Push the charger in fully.** A loose charger flickers on bumps and can heat up.
- **If the socket stops working,** the fuse for it may have blown. Have it checked and replaced with the correct fuse before using the socket again.

# Parked cars and the car battery

Some sockets stay live with the engine off. A phone left charging for hours can slowly drain the car battery, which is the last thing you want at a lodge upcountry.

- Unplug the charger when you park for a long time.
- If your car charger has a light that stays on after you switch off, your socket is always live.

# Use a well-made car charger

A car charger sits close to the driver for hours. A cheap, badly built one may overheat or give an unstable output. Signs of a problem:

- It gets too hot to touch.
- It smells of hot plastic.
- The plastic softens or discolours.
- The phone connects and disconnects repeatedly.

# Quick safety list

- Plug in after the engine starts.
- Keep the phone in the shade.
- Route the cable away from the driver.
- Unplug when you park.
- Replace anything that gets hot or smells.
