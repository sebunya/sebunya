On most Android phones with a card slot, you can **set the camera to save to the memory card** and **move existing photos and videos across** with the Files app. Moving apps is more limited: some phones and apps allow it, many do not. Menu names differ between brands, so look for the closest match on your phone.

Before you start, check your phone has a microSD slot (usually in the SIM tray) and the largest card it supports, in the manual or the maker's website.

# Step 1: put the card in

1. Switch the phone off, or use the phone's option to unmount storage if it has one.
2. Open the SIM tray with the ejector pin and place the card in the microSD space, the right way up.
3. Switch the phone on. You should see a notification that a card was found, or the card under Settings, then Storage.

If the phone asks how to use the card, choose **portable storage** for now. More on the other option below.

# Step 2: save new photos to the card

1. Open the Camera app.
2. Open its settings (often a gear icon).
3. Look for **Storage location** or **Save to** and choose **SD card**.

From now on, new photos and videos go straight to the card. If your camera app has no such option, you can still move them across from time to time with Step 3.

# Step 3: move the photos you already have

1. Open your phone's **Files** or **File Manager** app.
2. Go to internal storage, then the **DCIM** folder, then **Camera**.
3. Select the photos and videos you want to move. You can usually select all.
4. Choose **Move** (not Copy), then pick the SD card as the destination.
5. Wait for it to finish before using the phone heavily or removing the card.

Do the same for the **Download**, **Music** and **Movies** folders if they are large. Your Gallery app will still show the photos; it reads from the card too.

> Move a few files first and check they open from the card before moving everything.

# Step 4: apps

Some phones let you move an app to the card from **Settings**, then **Apps**, then the app, then **Storage**, then **Change**. If you do not see a Change button, the phone or the app does not allow it. Many apps, especially those that need to start quickly or run in the background, stay in internal storage.

An app moved to the card stops working if you remove the card.

# Formatting a card as internal storage

Some Android phones offer to **format the card as internal storage** (sometimes called "adoptable storage"). The card then acts as part of the phone's own storage. Before you choose this:

- **Formatting erases everything** on the card.
- **The card is locked to that phone.** It cannot be read in a computer or another phone afterwards.
- **Removing the card** can break apps and lose data stored on it.
- **A slow card slows the phone.** Choose a card with a good speed class.

For most people, portable storage is simpler and safer.

# Keep a backup

A memory card can be lost, damaged, or fail like any storage. Copy important photos to a computer, a [flash drive](/storage/usb-flash-drives) or the cloud now and then.
