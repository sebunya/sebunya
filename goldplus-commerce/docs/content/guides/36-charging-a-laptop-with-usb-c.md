Many newer laptops can charge from a USB-C charger that supports USB Power Delivery (PD). To do it reliably you need three things to line up: **a laptop that accepts power over USB-C, a charger that gives enough power on that port, and a cable rated for that power.** If any one is short, the laptop charges slowly, only charges when switched off, or does not charge at all.

# Check 1: does your laptop charge over USB-C?

Not every laptop with a USB-C port can charge through it.

- **Look at the original charger.** If it ends in a USB-C plug, your laptop charges over USB-C.
- **Look at the port.** Some makers print a small charging or plug symbol next to the USB-C port that accepts power.
- **Check the manual or spec page** for "USB-C charging" or "Power Delivery".

If your laptop came with a round barrel-plug charger, it may still have a USB-C port for data only. Check before you buy anything.

# Check 2: how much power does it need?

Turn the original charger over and read its output line. Volts times amps gives the watts. Laptops commonly use chargers in the range of about 45W to 100W, but yours may be different.

What happens if you use less:

- **A little less:** the laptop usually charges, more slowly.
- **Much less:** it may only charge while switched off or asleep, or slowly lose charge while you work.
- **Far too little:** it may refuse to charge and show a warning.

A charger with more power than the laptop needs is fine. The laptop takes only what it asks for.

# Check 3: the charger's output on the port you use

For a laptop, the details on the charger's label matter:

- The **USB-C port** must support **PD**.
- Find the output lines for that port. Laptops usually charge at **20V**, so look for a 20V line and multiply it out: 20V⎓3.25A is 65W.
- On a **multi-port charger**, the full figure is often available only when the laptop is the only thing connected. Plugging in a phone as well can lower what the laptop gets. Read the combined line.

# Check 4: the cable

The cable is easy to overlook.

- Most USB-C to USB-C cables are built for up to **3A**, which allows up to **60W**.
- Above 60W, PD needs **5A**, and only a cable built and marked for it can carry that.
- A cable made for phones may be rated lower still. Check the listing or the marking.

# Good habits

- **Use the laptop's own charger at your main desk** if it works, and keep the USB-C charger for travel.
- **Plug into the right port.** If your laptop has several USB-C ports, only some may accept charging.
- **Watch the battery icon** when you first try a new charger. Most laptops show whether they are charging, and some warn when the charger is too weak.
- **Unplug during power cuts** and wait for the supply to settle before plugging back in.
