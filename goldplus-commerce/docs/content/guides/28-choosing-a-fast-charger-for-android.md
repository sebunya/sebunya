There is no single "Android charger". Samsung, Tecno, Infinix, itel, Xiaomi and others use different fast-charging methods, and some phones support more than one. To choose well, find three things from your phone's specification: **the port, the fast-charging standard and the maximum charging power.** Then pick a charger and cable that match.

If the charger and phone do not share a standard, the phone still charges safely, just at normal speed.

# Step 1: find your exact model

Open Settings, then About phone, and note the model number, for example a code like KF6n, X695 or SM-A326B. The name on the box can cover several versions, and they do not always charge the same way.

With the model number, look up the phone's official specification page, or read the label on the charger that came in its box.

# Step 2: check the port

- **USB-C (Type-C):** the oval, reversible connector on most newer phones.
- **Micro-USB (often called V8):** the older, flat connector that only goes in one way.

Micro-USB phones cannot use USB PD. They charge from a USB-A port with a USB-A to Micro-USB cable. Our braided [GP-L14V V8 cable](/products/goldplus-cable-gp-l14v) is one option for these phones.

# Step 3: find the fast-charging standard

The spec page or the original charger will usually name it. Common ones are:

- **USB Power Delivery (PD)**, sometimes with **PPS**. Samsung's Super Fast Charging uses PD with PPS. Many other newer phones support PD too.
- **Quick Charge (QC)**, from Qualcomm, common on phones with Snapdragon chips, often over a USB-A port.
- **Brand methods.** Some makers, including Tecno and Infinix on some models, use their own fast-charging systems. These may only reach full speed with that brand's charger, and fall back to a standard speed with others.

If your phone's page says PD, look for PD on the charger's USB-C port. If it says QC, look for QC. If it names a brand system, a PD or QC charger will still charge it, possibly not at the very top speed.

# Step 4: check the power

The spec page normally gives a maximum, such as 18W or 33W. A charger that lists at least that much on the right port, in the right standard, gives your phone room to charge at its best.

A charger with more power than your phone needs is safe. The phone draws only what it can handle. More power is useful when one charger serves several devices.

Do not rely on a number printed large on a box. Read the output lines on the charger label for the port you will use.

# Step 5: match the cable

- **USB-C port on the charger to a USB-C phone:** a USB-C to USB-C cable.
- **USB-A port on the charger to a USB-C phone:** a USB-A to USB-C cable, such as our [GP-L14T](/products/goldplus-cable-gp-l14t), listed at 6A.
- **USB-A port to a Micro-USB phone:** a USB-A to Micro-USB cable.

A worn cable is one of the most common reasons a fast charger seems slow. Replace any cable with a loose plug or damaged sleeve.
