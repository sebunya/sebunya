Power cuts are part of life in many parts of Kampala and upcountry. The outage itself does little harm to a phone charger. The risk is the moment the power comes back, when the supply can briefly spike or flicker. The simplest protection is to unplug chargers when the power goes, and plug them back in once it has settled.

# What happens when the power returns

When mains power is restored, the voltage can jump or dip for a moment as the network and nearby equipment come back on. A short surge like this can stress the parts inside a charger, and in bad cases damage a charger or what is connected to it.

Well-made chargers have some protection built in, but they are not designed to absorb every spike. A few habits reduce the risk a lot.

# Habits that help

## Unplug during an outage

When the lights go off, unplug your chargers, or switch them off at the socket. Wait a few minutes after the power comes back before plugging in again, especially if the lights flicker.

## Use a surge-protected extension

An extension socket with built-in surge protection adds a layer between the wall and your chargers. Check the box or label for surge protection specifically; not every extension has it. Keep it off the floor where water can reach it.

## Do not leave phones charging unattended overnight during unstable power

If the power has been going on and off all evening, unplug before you sleep, and charge in the morning instead.

## Watch for warning signs

A charger that buzzes, smells of burning, has scorch marks or got very hot after the power came back has been damaged. Stop using it.

# Generators and inverters

Many homes and shops switch to a generator or inverter during an outage.

- **Generators:** small generators can give a less steady supply, especially when large loads switch on and off. Let the generator run for a moment before plugging in phones, and avoid sharing an extension with heavy appliances.
- **Inverters and solar systems:** a good inverter usually gives a steady supply suitable for chargers. Check its rating and do not overload it.
- **Changeover moments:** the switch from mains to generator, or back, is another moment for a surge. Unplugging during the changeover is a good habit.

# Keep a power bank charged

The best way to stay connected through a long outage is not to need the wall at all.

- Charge a power bank whenever the power is steady, so it is full when the lights go.
- Charge the phone from the power bank during the outage.
- Recharge the power bank once the supply has settled.

# Quick checklist

- Unplug when the power goes.
- Wait for the supply to settle before plugging back in.
- Use a surge-protected extension.
- Keep a power bank charged.
- Replace any charger that smells, buzzes or got very hot.
